How reliable is reliable enough?

Saved answers matter more to learners than a convenient reliability percentage. Define eligible events, user needs and a response policy before adopting the target.

01Three terms from the ground up

An SLI defines the measured behavior, such as successful saves divided by eligible attempts. An SLO sets a target and observation window. An SLA is a contractual commitment with specified consequences. Those terms should not be treated as interchangeable.

The 99.5 percent over 28 days example illustrates the shape of an objective, not Quickturn's universal required target. Define what counts as good and which events enter the denominator before choosing a percentage.

02The error budget

The error budget is the allowed fraction of unsuccessful events: 100 percent minus the objective. A team can agree in advance to reduce risky changes when that budget is depleted. With one million eligible requests and a 99.5% objective, the allowed unsuccessful count is 5,000.

Higher reliability consumes engineering work, and users notice some failure modes more than others. Quickturn prioritizes answers being saved and cards being available. Its device-side timer does not make lost saves harmless.

Measure the baseline and user needs before selecting a target. A target should express the desired experience and guide improvement, rather than simply match whatever current performance makes easy.

03What an SLO costs

The cost is honest measurement and a response policy. A 4xx may be a legitimate refusal or a bug affecting all clients; a slow 200 may still be unusable. The SLI definition must consider those cases.

Use production learner traffic for the objective, with suitable safeguards for low volume. Budget-burn alerts detect a meaningful failure rate. The team must accept the operational consequences, including prioritizing reliability work when the budget policy requires it.

04Reliability for an AI agent

An agent can be available and still fail its task. Measure task completion under an explicit success rubric, escalation rate and end-to-end latency. Appropriate human escalation is not automatically a failure, so distinguish required escalation from an avoidable rescue.

Several individually fast calls can add up to a slow task. Service availability, task success and judged answer quality are complementary signals. Choose the measure that reflects what the user waits for and needs completed.

Summary in three lines

  1. An SLI measures chosen behavior, an SLO sets its target, and an SLA states contractual commitments.
  2. For this event ratio, the budget is 100% minus the objective. Failures consume it and influence release decisions.
  3. Measure the baseline, choose a target from user needs, and assess agents at the whole-task level.
