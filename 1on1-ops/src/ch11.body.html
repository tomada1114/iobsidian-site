What happens to a learner's data when things go wrong?

A bad write, a table deletion and a learner’s account deletion are different events. Each needs its own protection or repeatable recovery path.

01Different failures, different guards

Point-in-time recovery, deletion protection and stack retention address different failures. Recovery helps with corrupted data by restoring another table. Deletion protection blocks a delete until disabled. Retention protects an existing resource when its stack is deleted or replaced.

A bug writes wrong data
Point-in-time recovery, into a new table.
Someone deletes the table
Deletion protection refuses the call.
A stack would replace or remove it
A Retain policy keeps the table.
A learner asks to leave
Deletion and export, as features.

None substitutes for the others. Recovery does not keep the app online automatically, and retention does not undo a bad write. Account export and deletion are separate product operations that also need a designed path.

02Choices per stage

Quickturn sets retain-on-delete and retain-on-replacement in both stages, with production table protection and recovery enabled from creation. The available recovery window starts only after recovery is active.

Identity resources deserve separate care. A replacement pool issues new provider identities, requiring mapping migration to reach existing learners. It is not enough to restore table records. Tests check these stage-specific protections, while drills test the recovery procedure.

03The accepted risk

Development accepts losing the team's test history; that is a stated risk rather than an omitted setting. Production recovery retains recoverable copies for a configured period, so deletion and privacy descriptions must include that lifecycle.

A restore drill creates a protected replacement table and checks counts and representative records. It measures time and tests the planned cutover or repair process. Restore access must remain controlled because the new copy contains production information.

04Deletion and export as features

A learner-bound partition simplifies export and deletion, but the operation must follow pagination and batch limits and include identity mappings. Tests make it a repeatable feature rather than a script someone remembers to run.

Derived copies include exports, logs, embeddings and conversation memory. Maintain an inventory and an explicit retention or deletion policy for each. Restoring a backup must also respect deletions made after its recovery point. Keeping progress in the main record makes that inventory smaller without removing responsibility for the remaining copies.

Summary in three lines

  1. Recovery, deletion protection and Retain policies each guard against a different failure.
  2. Each guard is a per-stage choice; a skipped one is a risk written down, not an oversight.
  3. Deletion and export are tested features over one partition, and must reach every derived copy.
