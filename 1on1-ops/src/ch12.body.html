What has to be true before we open to the public?

After this page you can walk through a production readiness list, and say why each item is on it. You can also explain which things to build early, and how to record what you choose to do without.

About 9 min readThe whiteboard room

In this page

  • What changes when strangers arrive?
  • What's on the readiness list?
  • What do we do without, and why?
  • What does an internal AI agent need?

The whiteboard room. Maya has written "dev" and "prod" at the top of two columns, and hands Leo the pen.

01When strangers arrive

L

Leo: Honestly, I'd write the same things in both columns. Same code, same tests, same alarms. Isn't prod just dev with a different URL?

M

Maya: Who uses dev today?

L

Leo: The team. Mostly the owner, practising.

M

Maya: And who will use prod?

L

Leo: Learners. People we've never met.

M

Maya: So what arrives with them?

L

Leo: Their data. And… their expectations, I suppose.

M

Maya: And their abuse. Some strangers will try to break things, or sign up ten thousand fake accounts.

L

Leo: So prod is dev plus people we can't trust.

M

Maya: Plus people we owe something to. That's what the second column is about.

L

Leo: Then why not build all of that from day one?

M

Maya: Quickturn's rule was to build early what's hard to add later. Learner isolation, auth, the data shape, infrastructure as code, fast deploys.

L

Leo: And the rest?

M

Maya: Added in one production-guard phase, before launch.

L

Leo: Where do separate accounts go? That sounds hard.

M

Maya: It sounds hard, but it's a known shape. An AWS Organization, with a management account that holds only billing and identity.

L

Leo: And then a dev account and a prod account.

M

Maya: Right. People sign in through IAM Identity Center, not as an IAM user with a password and keys.

L

Leo: Can I read prod data to debug something?

M

Maya: No. Nothing reaches prod except the deploy workflow behind its approval. And development never reads prod data.

L

Leo: What about the root user of each account?

M

Maya: Root has MFA and no access keys. Everyone else uses short-lived credentials, so there's no long-lived key to leak.

02The readiness list

L

Leo: So what goes in the prod column?

M

Maya: Walk it with me. First, the prod stacks, from the same code, with the prod stage.

L

Leo: Deployed behind an approval, with an audit trail.

M

Maya: Next, abuse. WAF rate-based rules at the edge.

L

Leo: So one address can't hammer the API.

M

Maya: Right. Then email. Sign-up and recovery email go through SES, not Cognito's built-in sender.

L

Leo: Why change the sender?

M

Maya: The built-in one is meant for testing, with low limits. Real learners need their recovery email to arrive.

L

Leo: Then the data guards: recovery and deletion protection. And account deletion and data export.

M

Maya: Then the privacy policy and the terms. And alarms on API errors and budget thresholds.

L

Leo: Watched by whom?

M

Maya: By a person, closely, through the first weeks. An alarm nobody watches is decoration.

L

Leo: And the reliability targets and restore drills?

M

Maya: Both run in prod. The targets need real traffic, and the drills need real data to restore.

L

Leo: What about payments?

M

Maya: Live payments go on in prod too. That's one more reason nobody touches prod by hand.

Accounts
Dev: the dev account. Prod: its own account.
Who can sign up
Dev: the team. Prod: anyone, self sign-up on.
Deploy approval
Dev: none. Prod: a reviewer, with an audit trail.
Abuse limits
Dev: none. Prod: WAF rate-based rules.
Auth email
Dev: the built-in sender. Prod: SES.
Data guards and rights
Dev: Retain only. Prod: recovery, deletion protection, deletion and export.
L

Leo: That's a lot of differences for "the same thing with a new URL."

M

Maya: And one more: what we do when an alarm fires.

L

Leo: A runbook?

M

Maya: A short page per alarm. What it means, what to check first, and how to reduce the harm.

L

Leo: Written before the alarm fires.

M

Maya: At 2 a.m., nobody reasons well. The page does the thinking you did in daylight.

L

Leo: And a real incident?

M

Maya: Someone leads it and keeps a timeline. Then the fix, and then a blameless review, as the Google SRE book describes.

L

Leo: Blameless meaning nobody gets punished?

M

Maya: Meaning you look for what let the mistake happen, not who made it. People only tell the truth when it's safe to.

L

Leo: Does Quickturn have all that?

M

Maya: One runbook per alarm, and a short written incident process.

L

Leo: And learner isolation? That felt like the scariest item.

M

Maya: It was built early and is tested in every store. A leak between learners is the one failure no runbook can undo.

03What we do without

L

Leo: Is there anything we skip?

M

Maya: Cognito has paid threat protection. We turn it on when abuse is observed, not in advance.

L

Leo: Isn't that risky?

M

Maya: A little. The WAF rules already limit the obvious attacks. The paid tier waits for evidence it's needed.

L

Leo: What else?

M

Maya: There's no separate analytics database. Reporting runs on exports to S3.

L

Leo: And running prod beside dev?

M

Maya: It doubles the operating work, and part of the cost. The team accepts that, because prod holds learners' data.

L

Leo: So how do you decide what to skip?

M

Maya: Each choice has a cost and a benefit. Skip something, and you have less evidence the first time you need it. But the effort goes where the risk actually is.

L

Leo: And the senior part?

M

Maya: Writing each "not at our size" down, with its reason. Then it's a decision, not something forgotten.

L

Leo: So next year, someone can read why, and change their mind on purpose.

M

Maya: That's the point. A written "no" can be revisited. A forgotten one just surprises you.

04An internal AI agent

L

Leo: Would an internal AI agent need all this? Only employees use it.

M

Maya: The same review, plus some items of its own. Who does the agent act as, and what may each tool do?

L

Leo: Least privilege, per tool.

M

Maya: Then a spend limit, and a kill switch.

L

Leo: Evaluation before rollout?

M

Maya: And monitoring of task success after it. Plus logs that don't keep sensitive prompts forever.

L

Leo: And if it gets something badly wrong?

M

Maya: A human escalation path. Someone the user can reach, quickly.

L

Leo: What's the usual mistake?

M

Maya: Treating "internal" as "no review needed." Employees paste customer data into it on the first day.

L

Leo: So the logs fill with customer data, from a tool nobody reviewed.

M

Maya: Right. The review isn't about who the users are. It's about what the system can touch.

05Leo explains it back

M

Maya: Hand me back the pen. What's the difference between the columns?

L

Leo: Production isn't dev with a new URL. It's strangers' data and strangers' abuse.

L

Leo: We built the hard-to-retrofit things early. The easy guards came in one phase before launch: separate accounts, approvals, WAF, SES, recovery, deletion and export.

L

Leo: Every alarm has a runbook.

M

Maya: And the things we skipped?

L

Leo: We write down what we do without, like paid threat protection, with the reason.

M

Maya: Date each of those notes too. A reason that was true at a hundred learners may not be true at ten thousand.

Summary in three lines

  1. Production means strangers' data, abuse and expectations, not just a new URL.
  2. Build hard-to-retrofit things early; add accounts, approvals, WAF, SES and data guards before launch.
  3. Give every alarm a runbook, and write down each thing you do without, with its reason.
