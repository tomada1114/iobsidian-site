What has to be true before we open to the public?

After this page you can walk through a production readiness list, and say why each item is on it. You can also explain which things to build early, and how to record what you choose to do without.

01When strangers arrive

A team-only environment and a public service have different exposure. Public launch brings persistent learner data, account recovery, abusive traffic and operational promises. Quickturn establishes hard-to-add foundations early: isolation, identity, data design and repeatable deployment.

Before launch it adds production accounts, controlled access and the necessary operational guards. Separate accounts and short-lived credentials limit ordinary development reach, provided cross-account roles and emergency access are also governed.

02The readiness list

Production readiness includes reviewed stacks, abuse controls, reliable sign-up and recovery email, data recovery, export and deletion, privacy descriptions, and monitored error and cost alerts. An alarm needs a destination and someone responsible for responding.

Accounts
Dev: the dev account. Prod: its own account.
Who can sign up
Dev: the team. Prod: anyone, self sign-up on.
Deploy approval
Dev: none. Prod: a reviewer, with an audit trail.
Abuse limits
Dev: none. Prod: WAF rate-based rules.
Auth email
Dev: the built-in sender. Prod: SES.
Data guards and rights
Dev: Retain and user pool protection. Prod: those, plus recovery and table deletion protection.

A runbook states what the alarm means, the first checks and ways to reduce harm. An incident has a coordinator and timeline, followed by a review of system conditions that allowed it. Exercise recovery and notification paths before relying on them during a real failure.

03What we do without

Some controls may be deferred for a small product if the risks and revisit conditions are explicit. A paid threat-protection tier or separate analytics database is a choice to assess, not automatically a launch requirement or automatically unnecessary.

Development and production add operational cost. Spend that effort according to the data, exposure and obligations the service actually has. A recorded deferred decision can be reconsidered; an unnoticed missing capability provides no such warning.

04An internal AI agent

An internal AI agent needs the same review plus trusted acting identity, scoped tools, spending bounds, evaluation and task-success monitoring. It also needs a reachable human escalation path and controlled retention of sensitive content.

“Internal” does not mean harmless: employees may supply customer information on the first day. Assess what the system can read, change and disclose, and verify those boundaries before real users depend on it.

Summary in three lines

  1. Production means strangers' data, abuse and expectations, not just a new URL.
  2. Build hard-to-retrofit things early; add accounts, approvals, WAF, SES and data guards before launch.
  3. Give every alarm a runbook, and write down each thing you do without, with its reason.
