Why DynamoDB and not PostgreSQL?

Quickturn must isolate, export and delete learner-owned records while spending little when idle. Those needs guide its database comparison before product preferences.

01Drivers before products

Quickturn lists its drivers before comparing products: learner isolation, straightforward export and deletion, and low idle cost. It also needs manageable Lambda access, conditional writes and enough history to rebuild derived state.

These are application needs rather than a preference for SQL or NoSQL. The comparison is useful only if each candidate is judged against the same needs and operational assumptions.

02The options side by side

The candidates include DynamoDB on-demand, Aurora DSQL, Aurora Serverless v2 and RDS PostgreSQL. They differ in idle behavior, network and connection requirements, transaction semantics and local testing options.

A PostgreSQL-compatible service is not necessarily identical to PostgreSQL in every transaction or supported feature. A decision must check the actual engine, version, limits and required network path. Quickturn prefers DynamoDB’s learner-key model and its tested conditional commit contract for this workload. Other workloads can favor relational serverless services.

03What DynamoDB costs

DynamoDB requires a key design for each live read, exports for ad hoc reporting and maintenance of derived projections. A complete answer log helps repair projections, but a missed update can still cause drift.

Relational storage can be a faster starting point when access patterns are uncertain or many-to-many relationships need constraints. A SQL-fluent team’s exploratory reporting needs can also favor it. Quickturn's early local prototype used SQL; multi-learner operation introduced a different set of drivers.

04Both, and how to defend it

Relational and key-value databases can serve different workloads. Running both adds a second operational system and may require synchronizing two copies. Add the second store for an observed requirement.

Defend the chosen database by stating drivers, rejected alternatives and a revisit condition. For Quickturn, a history lifecycle or reporting need that diverges from learner records could justify a separate log table. Reopen the decision when a driver changes, rather than whenever a new product appears.

Summary in three lines

  1. Choose a database from written decision drivers, and judge every option against the same list.
  2. DynamoDB won on a structural learner boundary; its price is designing every question as a key first.
  3. Pick relational when questions are unknown or relations are rich, and write down what would make you revisit.
