Split what breaks often from what must never be lost

The API can be rebuilt; learner records cannot be recreated from its code. Separate lifecycles make deployment and recovery decisions clearer.

About 4 min readQuickturn case study

In this page

  • Stateful versus rebuilt often
  • Loose coupling
  • Stages, config and secrets
  • What the split costs

01Stateful versus rebuilt often

Split stacks by lifecycle. Quickturn's foundation owns the learner table and identity pool, while the application stack owns frequently rebuilt compute, static delivery and alarms. Durable data should not be casually coupled to every app release.

DeletionPolicy and UpdateReplacePolicy retention protect existing resources during stack removal or replacement. A new identity pool changes provider identities; the application must preserve or migrate their mapping to internal learners. Retaining an old pool does not automatically make a replacement usable.

Deletion protection and production point-in-time recovery add further safeguards. They reduce accidents but still require recovery and migration procedures.

02Loose coupling

CloudFormation exports provide checked cross-stack references, but imported exports cannot be freely removed or changed. Quickturn instead publishes foundation identifiers under stage-specific Parameter Store names.

That loosens CloudFormation coupling at the cost of a string contract and deployment ordering. Tests check which names the foundation writes and which names the application reads. Runtime and deployment checks must still detect missing or incorrect values.

03Stages, config and secrets

One infrastructure program produces development and production from a validated stage setting. Differences such as recovery, deletion protection, sign-up policy and approval are kept explicit and small.

Ordinary settings are parsed at startup. Secrets need restricted retrieval and appropriate rotation rather than appearing in readable templates or function settings.

Parameter Store and Secrets Manager serve different lifecycle needs and pricing models. Quickturn uses a secure parameter for a rarely changed client secret and retrieves it through a cache. Cache lifetime affects rotation visibility; a nominal TTL is not proof that every environment has already refreshed.

Custom deployment code that copies a secret also needs narrow permissions and logs that omit secret values.

04What the split costs

Two stacks require ordered deployment and maintenance of their connecting identifiers. Retained resources can outlive their stacks and continue billing. Removal must therefore be an explicit, reviewed cleanup rather than an incidental delete.

A throwaway prototype may not need the same split. For production learner data, isolating the durable resources reduces the reach of an app deployment failure. It does not justify claiming that data is never at risk.

Summary in three lines

  1. Keep stateful resources in a rarely changed stack, retained on deletion and on replacement.
  2. Named parameters loosen stack coupling but require a checked identifier contract.
  3. Build every stage from one app, and choose secret storage according to access and rotation needs.
