Why does our deploy have no AWS keys?

After this page you can explain IAM users, roles and least privilege. You can also describe how CI gets into AWS through OIDC, with no stored secret at all.

01Users and roles

An IAM user is a long-lived identity that can have passwords or access keys. A role is assumed for temporary credentials issued by STS. Quickturn favors roles and Identity Center sessions rather than stored long-lived deployment keys.

IAM begins with implicit denial; policies allow specific operations, and an applicable explicit denial can override an allowance. Root access is reserved for tasks that require it.

02Two sides of a role, and OIDC

A role has two distinct questions: its trust policy controls who may assume it, and its permission policies control what the resulting session may do. GitHub Actions can present an OIDC token rather than retrieve an AWS key from repository secrets.

The deployment trust checks audience and the exact repository and branch or environment subject. GitHub's id-token: write permission lets the job request that token. The resulting role is limited to assuming the intended CDK deployment roles; production adds an approval gate.

Lambda uses an execution role too. Quickturn limits it to the required table, queue and secret resources, with appropriate service conditions for decryption. The role limits AWS access even if application code attempts more.

03What least privilege costs

Least privilege creates maintenance: a new resource can require a new policy. Broad wildcards reduce those failures by also allowing unwanted actions. Focus review on production permissions and trust conditions, where the potential damage is greatest.

An overly broad OIDC subject can admit unintended workflows. Exact repository identity still matters: accepting all branches of the correct repository is different from accepting unrelated repositories. Treat trust, not merely the attached permission list, as part of the deployment boundary.

04Agents get roles too

A hosted agent also has an execution role for model and AWS service access. That role does not automatically determine which learner's application records a tool may read.

Quickturn checks both layers: AWS permissions allow the service operation, and application authorization binds it to the verified learner and granted capability. Granting table access is not a substitute for tenant isolation.

Summary in three lines

  1. Prefer roles with temporary credentials over users with long-lived keys, and give each identity only what it needs.
  2. A trust policy says who may assume a role; OIDC lets CI prove its identity with no stored secret.
  3. Spend the most care on trust policies and anything that reaches data; a role limits resources, not whose data.
