What am I actually responsible for on AWS?

A development mistake should not reach production learner data. Separate accounts reduce that risk, while service configuration and cross-account trust still need care.

01Regions and accounts

An AWS account contains resources, permissions and billing information. A Region is a geographic area containing several isolated Availability Zones. Many services are regional, while services such as IAM and CloudFront have global aspects.

Quickturn places its primary regional resources in Tokyo for its initial Japanese learners. Account boundaries and geographic placement solve different problems: one concerns ownership and access, the other latency, resilience and data-location requirements.

02Shared responsibility

Under shared responsibility, AWS secures the underlying infrastructure, while customers secure their data, code, identities and configuration. The exact division depends on the service.

Managed Lambda and DynamoDB reduce operating-system and database maintenance. They do not choose an execution role’s table access or establish whether a token is trusted. Application authorization still decides which learner records a caller may reach. Those remain application and account responsibilities.

03The account as a blast radius

Separate development and production accounts reduce the reach of ordinary development mistakes. Quickturn uses AWS Organizations with a small management account and separate member accounts, plus IAM Identity Center for short-lived human access. Root users have MFA and no everyday access keys.

The management account deserves particular care because organization guard policies do not constrain it in the same way as member accounts. Cross-account trust must also be controlled: an account boundary is not protection if broad roles deliberately cross it.

Separation adds setup and operational work. Check free-plan and organization eligibility rules before relying on credits. Billing benefits are separate from the need to protect production learner data.

04Pricing is part of the design

Pricing has both fixed and usage-driven components. Instances, load balancers and NAT gateways can cost money while idle. Lambda invocations, DynamoDB on-demand operations and storage activity mostly follow usage, though retained data and logs still cost money.

An idle development environment can be inexpensive with few fixed components. At sustained high load, provisioned or always-on capacity may be cheaper. Evaluate both zero traffic and realistic growth, rather than assume “serverless” means free.

Summary in three lines

  1. A region is where things run; an account holds resources, permissions, the bill and the damage of a mistake.
  2. AWS secures the cloud; your data, IAM and configuration stay yours, however managed the service is.
  3. Separate prod into its own account, and choose per-request pricing when idle cost matters most.
