Who pays for the tokens, and what stops a runaway bill?

After this page you can explain how every token is traced to a feature and a learner. You can also explain a per-learner ledger with reserve and settle, and how it differs from rate limits and a kill switch.

The morning after an AWS Budgets alert on the dev account.

01Attribution first

L

Leo: My experiment script retried against Bedrock in a loop all night. The dev account's budget alert fired this morning.

M

Maya: Did you stop it?

L

Leo: Yes, first thing. But it made me wonder. What stops one learner, or one bug, from doing the same in prod?

M

Maya: What would you guess?

L

Leo: Rate limiting. API Gateway throttling, or a WAF rule.

M

Maya: Those help with something else. We'll get there. First, a question: what did your loop cost?

L

Leo: I'd have to wait for the bill.

M

Maya: That's the second trap. The monthly bill tells you the total, late. It can't tell you what one graded answer costs.

L

Leo: So how do we know?

M

Maya: Every call records a cost record against the learner who caused it. Let me draw one.

L

Leo: And the per-feature picture?

M

Maya: Each feature calls through its own tagged application inference profile. Cost Explorer then splits Bedrock spend by feature.

L

Leo: So two views.

M

Maya: Per call, from logs: what one graded answer costs. Per feature, from the bill: the monthly picture.

L

Leo: The tags show up right away?

M

Maya: Up to a day later, and only from when they're activated. So we create them before a feature launches.

02The ledger

L

Leo: Knowing the cost doesn't stop anyone spending, though.

M

Maya: No. That's the ledger's job. It answers one question: how much billed work may this learner still use?

L

Leo: One item per learner?

M

Maya: One item per learner, holding a balance. Before any billed call, the job reserves money from it.

L

Leo: Reserves how much? We don't know the cost until the model answers.

M

Maya: The most it could cost. We know the input size, and we set the output cap, so the maximum is known.

L

Leo: And the reserve is just a write?

M

Maya: A conditional write. It succeeds only if the balance covers that maximum. The reservation is the permission to call.

L

Leo: What key does the reservation use?

M

Maya: The grading job's key. So a retried or replayed job finds its reservation and is charged once.

L

Leo: What if the job crashes between the call and the settle?

M

Maya: The reservation expires, and the money goes back. The learner is never charged for a result they didn't get.

L

Leo: So the same key guards the grade and the money.

M

Maya: Right. And the call can't exceed the reservation, because the output cap is part of the maximum.

L

Leo: What if a learner runs out?

M

Maya: The reserve fails, and the call doesn't happen. The answer stays ungraded, and the learner sees why.

L

Leo: Why reserve at all? We could charge after the call, when we know the real cost.

M

Maya: Then the check comes too late. A learner with one cent left could start a call that costs ten, and we'd pay the difference.

L

Leo: So the reservation is what makes the limit real.

M

Maya: Yes, it turns a limit on paper into one the database enforces.

L

Leo: Where does the balance come from? A subscription?

M

Maya: That decision isn't final. A monthly allowance and prepaid credits differ only in how the balance is refilled.

L

Leo: So the ledger doesn't care about pricing.

M

Maya: Right. Payments on the web and in the app stores are adapters that only top up the ledger. And they're idempotent too, so a repeated payment event adds credit once.

03Rate limits and the kill switch

L

Leo: Back to my guess. Why doesn't rate limiting protect the bill?

M

Maya: A rate limit counts requests per client per time window. It doesn't know what a request costs.

L

Leo: So ten huge answers could pass a limit that stops a hundred small ones.

M

Maya: Exactly. Throughput belongs at the edge: WAF rules, API Gateway throttling. Entitlement is business state, in the application.

L

Leo: And my loop in dev?

M

Maya: The last guard: an AWS Budgets action. When spend crosses the budget, it attaches a policy denying Bedrock to the whole account.

L

Leo: A kill switch.

M

Maya: For bugs, not for one learner. Budget data lags actual spend, so it fires late. Here are the three guards together.

L

Leo: What does the Budgets action cost?

M

Maya: Budgets without actions are free. The first two budgets with actions are free, then ten cents a day each, as checked in September 2026.

L

Leo: And it can act without a person?

M

Maya: It can apply the policy automatically, or wait for approval. For a runaway loop at night, automatic is the point.

04What the ledger costs

L

Leo: This ledger sounds like a bank.

M

Maya: It's money-like state, so it's tested like one. Concurrent reserves, expiry and replays each have tests.

L

Leo: Concurrent reserves?

M

Maya: Two jobs for the same learner reserve at the same moment. The conditional write lets only as many through as the balance covers.

L

Leo: Anything else?

M

Maya: One more write per billed call. And reserving the maximum can refuse a learner who could have paid the actual cost.

L

Leo: Because their balance covers the real cost, but not the worst case.

M

Maya: Right. We accept that. And the kill switch stops everyone at once, which is the price of catching bugs.

L

Leo: Have you seen this go wrong somewhere else?

M

Maya: At my last company, an internal coding agent ran on one shared key. A CI job looped over a repository all weekend.

L

Leo: Like my script, but bigger.

M

Maya: It spent a month's budget. The alert only watched the total, so it fired on Monday.

L

Leo: What did they change?

M

Maya: Three things. A profile and a budget per team, and a token cap per run.

L

Leo: And the third?

M

Maya: An alert on the rate of spending, not only the total.

05Leo explains it back

M

Maya: Say how we'd stop your loop from happening in prod.

L

Leo: Every call is tagged, so we can say what a feature costs and what one answer costs.

L

Leo: Before a call, we reserve the maximum from the learner's balance under the job key. After, we settle the actual cost, so a retry is charged once.

L

Leo: Rate limits at the edge count requests. The ledger counts money. A Budgets action is the account-wide kill switch.

M

Maya: And your script?

L

Leo: The kill switch would have stopped it, late. A token cap on the script would have stopped it early.

M

Maya: Watch the spending rate as well as the total. A loop shows up as a steep line long before the total looks alarming.

Summary in three lines

  1. Record every call's cost against a feature and a learner, and tag a profile per feature.
  2. Reserve the maximum before a call and settle the actual cost after, keyed by the job.
  3. Rate limits count requests, the ledger counts money, and a Budgets action stops the account.
