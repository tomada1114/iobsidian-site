Who pays for the tokens, and what stops a runaway bill?

After this page you can explain how every token is traced to a feature and a learner. You can also explain a per-learner ledger with reserve and settle, and how it differs from rate limits and a kill switch.

About 4 min readQuickturn case study

In this page

  • How does a ledger bound each learner's spending?

01Attribution first

Record usage per call before trying to control it. Quickturn associates model and prompt versions, token categories, latency and estimated cost with the task and learner that caused the call.

Tagged application inference profiles support feature-level billing attribution. Those delayed reports answer a different question from call telemetry. Activate tags before relying on future costs to appear under them.

02The ledger

The usage ledger reserves an upper bound before starting billed work. A conditional write succeeds only if the learner's balance can cover the reservation. Input size, output cap and the applicable price rules must be included in the estimate.

A stable job key identifies the reservation and eventual settlement. Successful work replaces the estimate with actual usage; failed or abandoned work needs an explicit reconciliation policy. Expiry alone does not prove that the provider performed no paid work.

Refills, including payments or monthly allowances, must also be idempotent. The ledger controls learner entitlement while the application separately bears and measures provider costs.

03Rate limits and the kill switch

A request rate limit measures throughput, not token spending. Edge throttles help control traffic; application reservations control entitlement; an account-wide budget action contains wider failures.

Billing lag means the account switch can activate after additional spend has occurred. It is a last resort, with a tested policy and recovery procedure, rather than an exact real-time ceiling. The three controls operate on different signals and should be used together.

04What the ledger costs

Ledger writes, concurrency tests, expiry handling and replay protection add real work. Reserving the maximum can reject a call whose eventual actual price would have been affordable. That conservative trade prevents promising work before funds are available.

The account switch can stop legitimate use for everyone. Alerts on spending rate and per-run token limits help identify a runaway experiment sooner than a delayed monthly total. Test those limits as operational behavior, not only as arithmetic.

Summary in three lines

  1. Record every call's cost against a feature and a learner, and tag a profile per feature.
  2. Reserve the maximum before a call and settle the actual cost after, keyed by the job.
  3. Rate limits count requests, the ledger counts money, and a Budgets action stops the account.
