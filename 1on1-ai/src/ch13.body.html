Can a machine review the cards a machine wrote?

After this page you can explain how a pipeline replaces content work done by hand. You can also explain how an automated reviewer earns trust, and why the rejections it catches matter most.

01Where it started

Quickturn's early card process combined model generation, format and duplicate checks, human review and a publication stamp. Rejected cards retained specific reasons, such as unnatural phrasing or a missing valid alternative.

Those records provide evaluation material for a later automated reviewer. They do not establish that a second model must be capable of replacing the person who reviewed them.

02The pipeline

A state machine coordinates generation, deterministic checks, review and publication decisions. Step Functions Standard workflows retain execution history and support long-running orchestration. Explicit retries can execute tasks again, so downstream writes and publication steps still need idempotency.

Code chooses catalog gaps and requested card counts. The model does not independently decide what the curriculum needs. Supported batch inference can process noninteractive work, and failed records are handled separately.

Near-duplicate detection catches cards that differ only superficially. Workflow and model pricing must be checked for the chosen Region; the dated examples show meters rather than permanent quotes.

03Trusting the reviewer

Calibrate the reviewer on accepted cards and rejected cards with human reasons. A dataset dominated by accepts can make an always-accepting reviewer look strong, so false acceptance of known defects is a critical measure.

Check reasons as well as labels. Agreement on Japanese-to-English data does not prove quality for a language pair the team cannot read. That pair needs qualified evidence and audit before autonomous publication. Where a person can supervise, the machine can prioritize their attention rather than remove the review gate.

04Costs, and the same pattern elsewhere

The writer and reviewer may share blind spots, especially within the same model family. Different prompts or models help but do not guarantee independence. Sampled human audits and learner reports provide additional evidence.

Rerun evaluation when the reviewer changes and preserve new rejection reasons. Similar extraction workflows for invoices or claims can route uncertain items to a person. Historical human decisions become useful test data when their scope and quality are understood.

Summary in three lines

  1. Move content generation into a pipeline that records, retries and resumes each step.
  2. Calibrate review for each language pair, prioritize known defects, and retain qualified audit evidence.
  3. Guard against shared blind spots with a different reviewer prompt or model and sampled human audits.
