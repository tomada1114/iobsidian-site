01The exam

L

Leo: I understand AgentCore now: Runtime, Memory, Gateway, Identity, Policy, evaluations. Is that enough for the certification?

M

Maya: Which certification, exactly?

L

Leo: AWS Certified Generative AI Developer – Professional. AIP-C01.

M

Maya: It's the top-level certification for generative AI and agents. What do you know about the format?

L

Leo: Not much.

M

Maya: As of the research notes from 2026-09-17: 65 scored questions and 10 unscored ones. You pass with 750 or higher.

L

Leo: And the cost?

M

Maya: The fee was 300 dollars, as of the same notes. The certification stays valid for 3 years, and you can take it in Japanese.

L

Leo: When did AgentCore come into it?

M

Maya: An update to the standard version, published in March 2026, added AgentCore to the scope.

L

Leo: Who is it aimed at?

M

Maya: The recommended experience is at least 2 years with AWS and at least 1 year with generative AI.

L

Leo: And the topics?

M

Maya: About five domains, topic areas. Check the exact names and shares in the exam guide; I won't guess them.

L

Leo: So AgentCore is one part of it.

M

Maya: One part. It's easy to forget the exam also covers SageMaker widely.

L

Leo: I've never opened SageMaker.

M

Maya: Many agent developers haven't. That's exactly why it's worth knowing early, before the practice exam surprises you.

02Mapping what you know

Leo: How does what we've covered line up with the exam?

03What the exam adds

L

Leo: What's outside the map?

M

Maya: Several areas that appear often. SageMaker first: services for customizing, training and deploying models. We only looked at the Bedrock side.

L

Leo: Fine-tuning?

M

Maya: Model customization, yes. Training a model further to adjust how it behaves, when a prompt alone isn't enough.

L

Leo: What else?

M

Maya: Data preparation: collecting, formatting and checking the data used for training or search. It looks simple, but it directly affects accuracy.

L

Leo: And RAG? We talked about permissions.

M

Maya: The exam goes deeper. How you split documents into chunks, turn them into numbers called embeddings, and where you store them.

L

Leo: Where would you store them?

M

Maya: The choices include OpenSearch Serverless, Aurora with pgvector, and S3 Vectors.

L

Leo: Anything about calling models?

M

Maya: The inference modes in Bedrock: on-demand, provisioned throughput, cross-region inference and batch. Each is a different way to call a model and to be billed.

L

Leo: That's a lot.

M

Maya: Two more. Prompt management keeps prompts separate from code, and improves them with version control.

L

Leo: And the last one?

M

Maya: Model evaluation: comparing several models or prompts on the same standard, to choose one. That's different from agent evaluation, like AgentCore Evaluations.

L

Leo: How is it different?

M

Maya: Agent evaluation scores what one agent did in a conversation. Model evaluation compares candidates before you choose one.

L

Leo: So one is about behavior, and the other is about the choice.

04Working through S5

L

Leo: So how should I study? Start with a practice exam?

M

Maya: The certification sits last on your roadmap for a reason. The more hands-on work you do first, the less new material you have to memorize.

L

Leo: And the order within S5?

M

Maya: First, read the exam guide and mark what you already covered in stages S2 to S4.

L

Leo: Then?

M

Maya: Use the Udemy course to fill in only the parts you didn't mark. Then take the practice exam.

L

Leo: And memorize the answers I get wrong.

M

Maya: No. For each wrong answer, ask which part of your hands-on work explains it. That review raises your score fastest.

L

Leo: And if nothing I've done explains it?

M

Maya: Then that's a gap, and you do a small hands-on task for it. Reading alone is rarely enough for this exam.

L

Leo: So depth on AgentCore helps, but the exam wants breadth.

M

Maya: That's the trade. Our tutor needs depth, and the exam needs breadth. You'll spend a few weeks on areas you won't use this month.

L

Leo: Is the certification worth it, then?

M

Maya: What do you want it for?

L

Leo: Partly for me. Partly to show I can do this work.

M

Maya: Then think of it as a promotion exam, not a hiring exam. It checks whether you can explain, in words, the work you've actually done.

L

Leo: And at work?

M

Maya: It can help in an interview. But on a real project, what counts is explaining a whole design, from authentication to evaluation to operations, and why.

L

Leo: So the certification is an entry point, not proof I can design one.

M

Maya: Right. An entry point, not the goal.

L

Leo: Let me picture the agent as a new employee, and place every part.

M

Maya: Go ahead.

L

Leo: The learner shows a building pass, Identity, to enter a private room, Runtime. Inside are the new employee, Strands and a model, and a notebook, Memory.

M

Maya: And outside the room?

L

Leo: A front desk and rules, Gateway and Policy, lend tools and a workroom. Reports and reviews, Observability and Evaluations, record the work.

M

Maya: And you check the whole design, not only each part.
