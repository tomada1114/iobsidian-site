The certification map: AIP-C01, and what is outside this reader

This reader is meant to give you the big picture before you start hands-on work. It is not a study guide for a certification exam. Still, it helps to know where the topics in this reader overlap with the exam, and where they do not. This makes it easier to choose how to study next. In this last chapter, we use the AIP-C01 certification as an outer frame for the map, and look back at the whole reader.

01What is AIP-C01?

AWS Certified Generative AI Developer – Professional (AIP-C01) is the top-level certification in the field of generative AI and agents. It has 65 scored questions and 10 unscored questions. You pass with a score of 750 or higher. The exam fee is 300 dollars, and the certification stays valid for 3 years. You can take the exam in Japanese too.

An update to the standard version, published in March 2026, added AgentCore to the exam scope. The recommended experience is at least 2 years with AWS and at least 1 year with generative AI. It is easy to forget that the exam also covers SageMaker widely, not just AgentCore. The exam has about 5 domains (topic areas). We will not make up numbers for the name and share of each domain here. Check the exam guide (roadmap item 5-1) for that.

03What this reader does not cover

Some areas often appear on the exam but are not covered in this reader. Keep them in mind as areas outside this map, when you do hands-on work.

SageMaker
A group of services for customizing, training, and deploying models. This reader focuses only on the Bedrock side.
Model customization, such as fine-tuning
A way to adjust how a model behaves, by training it further. It is used when a prompt alone is not enough.
Data preparation
The process of collecting, formatting, and checking the quality of data used for training or search. It looks simple, but it has a direct effect on accuracy.
The details of RAG (chunking, embeddings, and vector stores)
The detailed design of how you split documents into chunks, how you turn them into numbers (embeddings), and where you store them. Choices for a vector store include OpenSearch Serverless, Aurora with pgvector, and S3 Vectors.
The different inference modes in Bedrock
The differences between on-demand, provisioned throughput, cross-region inference, and batch: different ways to call a model, and different ways you are billed for it.
Prompt management
A system for keeping prompts separate from your code, and improving them over time with version control.
Model evaluation
A system for comparing several models or prompts using the same standard, to decide which one to use. This is different from the agent evaluation this reader covers.

04How to work through S5

The certification sits at the end of the roadmap because the more hands-on work you do first, the less new material you need to memorize for the exam. In S5, follow this order.

First, read through the exam guide (5-1) and mark the parts you already covered in S2 to S4. Next, use the Udemy course (5-2) to fill in only the parts you did not mark. Last, take the practice exam (5-4). For each question you get wrong, do not just memorize the right answer. Instead, ask yourself: which part from S2 to S4 explains this? This kind of review, not memorizing, is the fastest way to raise your score.

05Looking back at the whole reader

Let's place all the parts of this reader back onto the new employee analogy, one more time.

Analogy: a promotion exam

AIP-C01 is not a hiring exam that a new employee takes before joining a company. It is closer to an internal promotion exam, taken some time after you start the job. It checks whether you have real hands-on experience, so memorizing alone is not enough to pass. If you have read this whole reader, think of this certification as a final step, to check whether you can explain, in words, the work you have actually done.

Where companies get stuck

Having this certification can help you in a job interview. But it is not what a company checks on a real project. What matters there is whether you can explain a whole design, from authentication to evaluation to operations, and why you designed it that way. A certification is only one entry point. It is not the goal.
