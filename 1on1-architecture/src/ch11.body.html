How do I defend a design decision in a review?

After this page you can write an architecture decision record that answers a reviewer before they ask. You can also keep it current as things change, and match a decision's effort to how hard it is to reverse.

01The record, and its best part

An architecture decision record, or ADR, explains a choice while it can still be challenged. The basic format records a title, status, context, decision and consequences. Quickturn also records decision drivers, considered options, open questions and dated sources.

The rejected options are particularly useful in review. “Microservices add operational cost without useful independence at our size” explains more than “we prefer a monolith.” Dated price or limit evidence lets future readers judge whether the decision's assumptions still hold.

02Status and change

A proposed record states a recommendation; an accepted record is a basis for implementation; a superseded record points to the decision that replaced it.

Many teams preserve accepted records unchanged and write a replacement when the decision evolves. Quickturn instead amends an accepted record in place, adding a dated explanation, and reserves superseding for a replaced decision. Git retains the historical versions.

The trade is readability versus historical browsing: the file states the current choice, while a reader must inspect diffs to reconstruct its development. Partial acceptance or replacement must identify exactly which part still applies.

03Effort matches reversibility

Decision effort should match the cost of reversal. A formatting library or dashboard arrangement may be easy to replace. A learner-isolation scheme or data model can require a migration of every record.

Quickturn therefore establishes hard-to-add foundations early and introduces some operational guards later. “Later” is appropriate only while their absence fits the current exposure and requirements. The useful distinction is whether adding a component later forces a redesign, not whether the component itself is effortless.

04Presenting a decision

Present a decision by stating its drivers, alternatives, consequences and a condition that would change it. A cache proposal should identify an observed performance problem and compare an in-process cache, a cache service and leaving the current design alone.

Quickturn's catalog is already a fixed snapshot built with the app. Before adding a cache, measure whether reading it is costly. A measurable trigger for revisiting the decision is stronger than a claim that caching must be faster.

Rejected alternatives make assumptions reviewable. A reviewer can challenge why an option lost, and the team can change course while that is cheap. ADRs themselves take time and become stale, so use them for choices a reasonable reviewer could dispute, rather than documenting every minor preference.

Summary in three lines

  1. A decision record states the context, the options rejected and why, and the consequences accepted.
  2. Keep accepted records current with dated amendments, and supersede them only when the decision is replaced.
  3. Spend decision effort in proportion to the cost of reversing, and lead a review with drivers and rejected options.
