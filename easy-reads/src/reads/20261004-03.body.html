The blank screen problem

Imagine two developers at the end of a long day. The first one finishes every task, closes the laptop and feels good. The second one stops in the middle of the work and writes a short note: "Next: write the test for the empty list."

The next morning, the first developer opens the laptop and sees a blank screen. There is nothing to pick up where you left off, because nothing was left. So this person reads old messages, checks the news and slowly tries to decide what to do. The second developer reads one line and can begin in a few minutes.

Why starting feels heavy

Starting is often harder than continuing. When you start, you have to remember where you were, choose a task and build up energy at the same time. Each of these small decisions has a cost, and together they can make you wait, look at your phone or do something easier instead.

A note from yesterday removes most of these decisions. You do not need to plan; you only need to follow your own sentence. The clearer the note is, the faster you can get going, which here means to start working and build some speed.

What makes a good note

Not every note works. "Next: improve the app" is too big and too vague, so it brings back the same blank-screen feeling. A useful note names one clear action that you can finish in about five minutes. The smaller the step is, the less resistance, the feeling of not wanting to start, you will feel.

Some people even stop before a task is done, so that the next step is already obvious. For example, you might stop after you have written the name of a function but before you write its body. This idea also works outside coding. A writer can stop in the middle of a paragraph, and a learner can write, "Next: review the five new words from Unit 3."

One sentence for tomorrow

Here is a small step you can try. Before you stop today, write one sentence that starts with "Next:" and names a step you can do in five minutes. Put it where you will see it first, such as the top of your notes or a sticky note on your screen.

Tomorrow's start can be prepared today, in one sentence. It costs you less than a minute, and it might save you half an hour of slow, unclear starting.

English to notice

So that: “Some people even stop before a task is done, so that the next step is already obvious.” This shows the purpose of an action.

Comparatives: “Starting is often harder than continuing.” “The clearer the note is, the faster you can get going.” The “the more ... the more” pattern shows that two things change together.

Think about it

What will your "Next:" sentence be for tomorrow? Try to explain it with “so that,” for example: “I will stop here so that I can get going quickly.”
