Do I need to talk a lot?

No. Many people think a good meeting needs long, smart speeches. But most good talks are made of small moves. A short question can help more than five minutes of talking.

In a team discussion, a talk where people share ideas, you can join with one or two sentences. Here are four small moves. Each one has a simple phrase you can use.

How do I agree and add?

Start from what someone just said. Then add one small thing. People often call this "Yes, and..." You do not fight the idea. You build on it.

You can say, "Can I add something?" or "Good point. And we could also test it first." These phrases are short and friendly. They show that you listened.

What if you do not agree? You can still be kind. Try "I see your point, but I have a worry." Then say your worry in one sentence.

What if I do not understand?

Ask a short question. Other people may have the same question. They are just quiet. "Sorry, what do you mean by that?" is a fine question. So is "Can you give an example?"

When you give your own view, keep it short too. Say what you think first. Then give one reason. For example, "I think we should wait. The tests are not ready yet."

How do we finish?

Many meetings go around and around. Nobody decides anything. Here, one person can help a lot. Try to sum up, or say the main points again in a few words.

You can say, "So are we saying we will wait one week?" Now the group can answer yes or no. This is a small move, but it moves the group to a decision.

You can also ask, "Who will do this, and by when?" Then everyone knows the next step. Small moves like these help the team, and they are easy to learn.

Words to notice

discussion
a talk where people share ideas
build on it
use an idea and add more to it
worry
a small fear that something may go wrong
sum up
say the main points again in a few words
decision
a choice that the group makes

Which of the four small moves is easy for you? Which one do you want to try in your next meeting?
