Life without a mouse

Most of us hold a mouse all day. We point, we click, and we do not think about it. But many people cannot use a mouse at all.

Some people have hands that shake. Some cannot move their arms well. Some people are blind, so they cannot see where to point. For all of them, the keyboard is the way in.

The Tab key does the walking

Open a web page and press the Tab key. You will see a box or a line move to the first link or button. This is called focus, the mark that shows which part will act next.

Press Tab again, and the focus moves to the next button. Press Shift and Tab together, and it goes back. When the focus is on a button, press Enter or the space bar to use it.

Inside a menu or a list, the arrow keys move up and down. The Esc key closes a menu or a pop-up window. With only these few keys, you can go almost anywhere.

Shortcuts save time

A shortcut, a group of keys you press together, does one job fast. You may know some already. Ctrl and C copies text. Ctrl and F finds a word on the page.

People who use only the keyboard learn many shortcuts. They often work faster than people with a mouse. Many engineers do the same thing for speed.

What good apps do

A good app lets you reach every button with the keyboard alone. The focus moves in a clear order, from top to bottom. You can always see where the focus is.

A bad app breaks this. Some buttons are only pictures, so Tab skips them. Sometimes the focus goes into a pop-up and cannot get out. This is called a keyboard trap, a place you can enter but not leave.

If you build apps, try this test. Put your mouse away and use your app for five minutes. You will find the problems fast. The fix is often small, like using a real button tag.

Words to notice

focus
the mark that shows which part of the screen will act next
shortcut
keys you press together to do one job fast
keyboard trap
a place you can enter with keys but not leave
shake
move quickly back and forth without wanting to
pop-up
a small window that opens on top of the page
