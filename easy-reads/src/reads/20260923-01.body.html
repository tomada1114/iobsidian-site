Life without a mouse

Most of us hold a mouse all day. We point, we click, and we do not think about it. But many people cannot use a mouse at all.

Some people have hands that shake. Some cannot move their arms well. Some people are blind, so they cannot see where to point. For all of them, the keyboard is the way in.

The Tab key does the walking

On a page with clear keyboard support, press the Tab key. The element receiving keyboard input has focus. A box or line around a link can show you which element that is. If you cannot see it, finding the next action becomes harder.

Press Tab again, and the focus moves to the next button. Press Shift and Tab together, and it goes back. When the focus is on a button, press Enter or the space bar to use it.

Some menus use arrow keys to move between choices, and Esc may close an open menu. The exact keys depend on the app. Try its help page if the usual keys do not work, rather than guessing that the same keys work everywhere.

Shortcuts save time

A shortcut, a group of keys you press together, does one job fast. You may know some already. Ctrl and C copies text. Ctrl and F finds a word on the page.

People who use only the keyboard learn shortcuts for tasks they repeat. A shortcut can save a trip to the mouse, but speed depends on the task and the person. Keyboard support matters even when it does not make a task faster.

What good apps do

A good app lets you reach every button with the keyboard alone. The focus moves in an order that makes sense for the task. You can always see where the focus is.

A control may look like a button but have no keyboard action. Tab can then skip it. Sometimes focus enters a pop-up and cannot get out. This is called a keyboard trap, a place you can enter but not leave.

If you build apps, put your mouse away and try one common task. Note any missing focus mark or action you cannot reach. A real button tag may fix one problem, while a pop-up may need its focus behavior changed.

Words to notice

focus
the state of receiving keyboard input
shortcut
keys you press together to do one job fast
keyboard trap
a place you can enter with keys but not leave
shake
move quickly back and forth without wanting to
pop-up
a small window that opens on top of the page
goes back / put your mouse away
return / stop using something for a while

English to notice

When clauses: “When the focus is on a button, press Enter or the space bar to use it.” When introduces the situation in which the action applies.

Think about it

What happens when you use only keys for one common task? Use a when clause to explain one step or problem.
