Is it a real question?

Q: Someone asks me "How are you?" and then walks away. Is that rude?

A: No, it is not rude. In the US, "How are you?" is often a greeting, a way to say hello. It is not always a real question. The person is being friendly. They do not expect a long answer.

You hear it in many places. A worker at a shop says it. A neighbor says it in the hall. A coworker says it in the morning. Most of the time, it means the same thing as "Hi."

The normal answer

Q: So what should I say?

A: Keep it short. The most common answer is "Good, thanks. How are you?" You can also say "Fine, thanks" or "Not bad." Then you ask the same question back. This small back and forth is the whole talk.

Many people say "Good" even on a bad day. This is not a lie in their eyes. It is part of the ritual, a set of words people say the same way each time. The words are about being polite, not about feelings.

When a long answer is fine

Q: Can I ever give a real answer?

A: Yes. With a close friend, "How are you?" can be a real question. The friend stops, looks at you, and waits. Then you can talk about your day. It also depends on the tone of voice and the place.

A quick clue is time. If the person is still walking, give the short answer. If they sit down with you, they may want more. A friend may also ask "How are you really?" That is a sign they want the real answer.

Why it feels strange

Q: Why does this feel so odd to me?

A: In many languages, a question like this is a real question. So a short reply can feel cold. But in the US, the short reply is warm. It says "I see you, and I am friendly."

Think of it like a ping between two computers. One side sends a small message. The other side sends a small reply. Nobody sends a full report. The goal is only to show that both sides are there.
