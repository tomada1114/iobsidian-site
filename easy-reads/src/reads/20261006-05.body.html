An hour on one test

Imagine a developer who is stuck on a failing test. She has read the error many times, and she has changed the code twice. An hour has passed, but the test is still red.

She thinks about asking a teammate. Then a quiet voice says, "If you ask, they will think you cannot do your job." So she keeps trying alone, and the problem stays the same.

Say what you tried

Many people ask for help like this: "The test is failing. I can't fix it." That message is honest, but it gives the helper very little. The helper must start from zero and ask many questions first.

Now compare it with this message: "I want the login test to pass. I tried clearing the cache, and I checked the test data. I think the problem is the date format." This version turns "I can't do it" into a clear question. The helper can see where to look, so the answer usually comes faster.

It also changes how asking feels. Although you still need help, the message shows the work you have already done. It does not show weakness. It shows effort, and it shows that you are thinking about the problem.

A simple rule for when

Some people ask too early, and some people wait far too long. A simple decision rule helps: set a time limit before you start. For example, give yourself 30 minutes. If you find the answer, great. If you are still stuck when the time is up, you reach out, which means you contact someone and ask.

The time limit removes the daily debate in your head. You do not need to decide again and again whether it is "okay" to ask. Even though asking may still feel a little uncomfortable, the rule makes the choice for you.

Three lines to send

When the time is up, write three short lines:

  • Goal: what you want to happen.
  • Tried: what you did and what happened.
  • Guess: your best idea about the cause.

Sometimes, while you are writing these lines, you notice the answer yourself. If not, send them. Next time you get stuck, try one 30-minute limit and one three-line message. Asking well is part of doing good work, not a sign that you are not good enough.

Words and phrases to notice

stuck on
not able to move forward with something. "I'm stuck on this bug."
reach out
contact someone, often to ask for help
time limit
the longest time you allow for something
decision rule
a simple rule that helps you choose
effort
hard work you put into something

English to notice

I tried / I have checked: “I tried clearing the cache, and I checked the test data.” Past simple tells finished actions. “The work you have already done” uses present perfect: past actions that matter now.

Although / even though: “Although you still need help, the message shows the work you have already done.” These words join two ideas that seem to go against each other.

Think about it

Think of a problem you are stuck on now. What have you tried so far, and who could you reach out to? Try using “I have tried” and “although” in your answer.
