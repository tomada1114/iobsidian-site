Not just wet skin

After a long bath, the skin on your fingers and toes gets wrinkly. For a long time, people thought the answer was easy. The skin drinks water, gets bigger and folds.

But that is not the whole story. The wrinkles show up only on your fingers and toes. Your arms and back stay smooth. And the wrinkles come in the same shape every time.

A clue from doctors

Many years ago, doctors noticed something strange. Some people had a hurt nerve in one finger. A nerve is like a wire that carries messages from your body to your brain. When that nerve was cut, the finger did not get wrinkly in water.

So the wrinkles need a working nerve. This showed that your body makes the wrinkles on purpose. It is not just water doing it.

What happens under the skin

Under your skin, there are many small blood vessels, tiny tubes that carry blood. When your hands stay in water, your nerves send a signal. The blood vessels in your fingers get smaller.

Now there is less space under the skin. The skin on top stays the same size, so it has to fold. That makes the wrinkles.

When you get out of the water, the vessels open again. After a few minutes, your fingers are smooth.

Here is a simple test. Put one hand in warm water for ten minutes and keep the other hand dry. Then look at them side by side. Only the wet hand has the folds.

Why would the body do this?

Nobody knows for sure. One idea is that wrinkly fingers help us hold wet things. The folds may work like the lines on a car tire, which move water out of the way on a wet road.

Some small tests found that people moved wet stones a little faster with wrinkly fingers. Other tests found no change. So the idea is interesting, but it is not proven yet.
