A short meeting to start the day

It is ten in the morning. Five people on a software team meet near a big screen. Nobody sits down. This is the stand-up, a short meeting at the same time every day.

People stand so the meeting stays short. Tired legs help everyone keep it quick. Many teams try to finish in about fifteen minutes. Some teams now meet on a video call, but they still call it a stand-up.

The same three questions

Each person speaks in turn. They answer three short questions. What did I do yesterday? What will I do today? Is anything blocking me?

Mia goes first. "Yesterday I fixed the login bug. Today I will write tests for it. Nothing is blocking me." That takes about twenty seconds.

Ken goes next. "Yesterday I worked on the new search page. Today I will keep going. But I am blocked. I need a key for the test server." A blocker is anything that stops your work. You cannot fix it alone, so you say it out loud.

Save long talks for later

Mia knows about the test server. She starts to explain how it works. The team lead stops her in a kind way. "Good point. Let's take that offline." In a meeting, take it offline means "talk about it later, with fewer people."

This is the second rule of a stand-up. Long talks move to after the meeting. Only the people who need the talk stay for it. Everyone else goes back to work.

Some teams call this time the parking lot. You "park" a topic during the meeting. Then you come back to it when the stand-up ends.

Why it works

The stand-up is not a report to the boss. It is for the team. In a few minutes, everyone knows who is doing what. Problems come out early, before they grow. After the meeting, Ken and Mia talk for five minutes, and Ken gets his key.

Words to notice

stand-up
a short team meeting every day, often standing

Think about it

Try the three questions for yourself. What did you do yesterday, what will you do today, and is anything blocking you?
