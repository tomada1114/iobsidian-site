A small town

Think of a small town with five people. Some of them trust each other. You want to know one thing. Is there a person that everyone trusts?

You could ask each person many questions. But there is an easier way. You can draw a picture and count.

Dots and arrows

Draw each person as a dot. When A trusts B, draw an arrow from A to B. This picture is called a graph. In a graph, the dots are called nodes, and the arrows are called edges.

The arrow has a direction. A trusts B does not mean B trusts A. So a graph with arrows like this is called a directed graph.

Now look at one dot. Some arrows come into it. Some arrows go out of it. Those are two different numbers.

Arrows in, arrows out

The number of arrows coming in is the in-degree. It tells you how many people trust this person. The number of arrows going out is the out-degree. It tells you how many people this person trusts.

In our town, say the baker has four arrows coming in. Every other person trusts the baker. And the baker has no arrows going out. The baker trusts no one in this picture.

So the baker has an in-degree of four and an out-degree of zero. With five people, that is the most an in-degree can be. Nobody else can match it.

Why counting is enough

You do not need to follow long paths in the graph. You only go through the list of arrows once. For each arrow, add one to the "in" count of the person at the end. Add one to the "out" count of the person at the start.

Then look for a person with in-degree of everyone else and out-degree zero. There can be only one such person. If two people had it, they would need to trust each other. But then their out-degree would not be zero.

In code, this is two small arrays and one loop. Many coding practice sites have this as a problem, often called "find the town judge."

Words to notice

graph
dots joined by lines or arrows
directed graph
a graph where each arrow points one way
in-degree
the number of arrows coming into a dot
out-degree
the number of arrows going out of a dot
trust
to believe that someone is good and honest

Think about it

Think of a group you know, like a team or a class. Who would have many arrows coming in? Why?
