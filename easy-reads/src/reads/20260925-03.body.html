Out to the farm

It is a cool Saturday in October. The leaves are red and yellow. A family gets in the car and drives out of town. They are going to a pumpkin patch, a farm field where pumpkins grow.

This is a common fall trip in the US. Many families go every year. Some farms are small and quiet. Others are busy and have games, food and music.

At the gate, the family parks on the grass. Kids run ahead. The air smells like dry leaves and warm apple cider, a sweet apple drink.

A ride on the hay

First, the family gets on a hay ride. A tractor pulls a long, open cart. The cart has big blocks of hay to sit on. Hay is dry grass that farms use to feed animals.

The ride is slow and a little bumpy. The cart goes past fields, trees and a few cows. The kids laugh each time the cart hits a bump. At the end of the ride, the cart stops next to the pumpkin field.

Lost in the corn

Next to the field is a corn maze. A maze is a set of paths that is hard to find your way through. Here, the walls are tall corn plants. They are much taller than an adult.

The family walks in. They turn left, then right, then left again. Soon they hit a dead end and walk back. Some farms give you a map. Others let you find your own way. After a while, the family finds the exit. Everyone is happy and a little tired.

Picking the right pumpkin

Now it is time for the main event. The pumpkins sit on the ground in long rows. Some are big and round. Some are small, tall or not the same on each side. Each kid walks up and down the rows to find the right one.

This takes time. One pumpkin is too heavy. Another has a soft spot. At last, each kid picks one. They carry it to a small shop and pay for it before they go home.

Why do people want a pumpkin? Many will carve it, which means to cut shapes into it. At home, they cut off the top and take out the seeds. Then they cut a face into the side and put a light inside. This is a jack-o'-lantern. People put them outside their doors for Halloween, at the end of October.
