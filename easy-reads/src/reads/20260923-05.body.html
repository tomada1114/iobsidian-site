An empty pocket

It was a cold Monday morning. Ben stood at the bus stop with his coffee in one hand. The bus came, and the doors opened with a soft sound.

He stepped up and put his hand in his coat pocket. There was nothing there. His coins were on the kitchen table at home.

On a city bus, the fare is the money you pay for one ride. Ben needed two dollars and fifty cents. He had a card for the store, a key and a receipt. That was all.

Two people help

"Sorry," Ben said to the driver. "I forgot my money. I can get off." The people behind him waited. His face felt hot.

An old woman in the first seat looked up. "Wait," she said. She took out a small card. "This is my bus pass. A pass lets you ride many times. But it only works for me."

She smiled and opened her bag again. She found some coins and held them out. "Here. Take these."

The driver shook his head. "Put those away, ma'am. Sir, just sit down. You can pay me next time. I drive this route every day."

Ben thanked them both. He sat down near the back. The woman put her coins back in her bag.

Next stop

The bus moved through the busy streets. A voice from a speaker said, "Next stop, Main Street." Ben knew his stop was the one after that.

On many American buses, you pull the cord to ask the driver to stop. The cord is a thin rope that runs along the windows. When you pull it, a bell rings and a sign lights up.

Ben pulled the cord. The bell rang. He walked to the front and said thank you again.

The next morning

The next day, Ben got on the same bus. He had his coins ready. He also had a second coffee in his hand.

He paid his fare and one more fare for yesterday. Then he gave the coffee to the old woman. She laughed. "How did you know I would be here?" she asked.

The driver laughed too. "She is here every day," he said. "Like me. She rides this bus to the end of the line and back."

"Why?" Ben asked. The woman held up her coffee. "Because people are nice on this bus," she said.
