Do I need to say goodbye?

Yes. In most English-speaking offices, people say goodbye when they leave a team. They do not just stop coming. Even a small goodbye matters.

The most common way is a farewell message, a short note to say goodbye. People send it by email or in the team chat. It often goes out on the last day or the day before.

What goes in the message?

Keep it short and warm. Five or six sentences are enough. Start with the news: "Today is my last day on the team."

Then say thank you. The best notes thank people by name. For example: "Thanks, Sam, for helping me with my first big release." One real detail like this means more than a long list.

Do not complain about the job or the company. Your note may stay in people's inboxes for years. Keep it kind and simple.

Should you write to the whole company? Usually not. Write to your own team and the people you worked with most. If you worked closely with one person, you can also send them a short private note.

How do I keep in touch?

End the note with a way to reach you. Many people add a personal email or a link to their work profile. You can write, "Let's keep in touch." It means "Let's stay friends and talk sometimes."

This is not only polite. People who change jobs often meet again later. A past teammate may help you find your next job, or you may help them.

What happens on the last day?

On the last day, you often finish a handoff, the work of passing your tasks to other people. You write notes so the team can do your work without you.

Some teams have a small party or a lunch. Some teams send a card with short notes from everyone. If your team does this, say thank you again. Then you log off for the last time.
