White light has many colors

Sunlight looks white to us. But white light is really a mix of many colors. You can see them in a rainbow: red, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet.

Each color is a kind of wave. Red light has long waves. Blue light has short waves. This small difference is the key to the colors of the sky.

Air sends blue light everywhere

Air is made of very small parts called molecules. When sunlight hits them, some of the light bounces off in a new direction. This is called scattering. It means light is sent out in many directions.

Air scatters blue light much more than red light. The short blue waves hit the molecules and fly off all over the sky. The long red waves mostly go straight through.

So when you look up in the day, blue light comes to your eyes from every part of the sky. That is why the whole sky looks blue. The sun itself still looks white or a little yellow.

A longer road at sunset

At noon, the sun is high. Its light takes a short path through the air to reach you. At sunset, the sun is low, near the horizon, the line where the sky meets the land.

Now the light comes in from the side. It must travel through much more air before it reaches your eyes. On this long road, the air scatters most of the blue light away. Very little blue is left by the end.

Red and orange light do not scatter as much. They keep going and reach you. So the sun and the clouds near it turn red, orange and pink.

One rule, two colors

The blue day sky and the red sunset come from the same rule. Air scatters blue more than red. In the day, we see the scattered blue. At sunset, we see the red and orange that are left.

Dust and smoke in the air can also change the colors. On some days, sunsets look even deeper red.

Words to notice

molecules
very small parts that air and other things are made of
scattering
sending light out in many directions
scatters
sends something out in many directions
horizon
the line where the sky meets the land or sea
bounces off
hits something and goes in a new direction
