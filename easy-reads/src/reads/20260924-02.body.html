What is the last question?

Q: How do most interviews end?

A: Near the end, the interviewer often says, "Do you have any questions for us?" Many people say, "No, I think you covered everything." That feels polite. But it is a lost chance.

This question is not only a formality, something people say just to be nice. The interviewer wants to see what you care about. Your questions tell them a lot about you.

Questions to skip

Q: Are there questions I should not ask?

A: Yes. Do not ask things you can find on the company website. "What does your company make?" is a bad question. "Where is your main office?" is also a bad one. These show that you did not do your homework before the interview.

Also, be careful with questions only about you. "How many days off do I get?" is a fair thing to want to know. But it is better to ask it later, after you get an offer.

Questions that work

Q: So what should I ask?

A: Ask about the team and the real work. The best questions have answers you cannot find online. Only people inside the company know them. Here are some good ones.

"What does a normal week look like on this team?" "What is the biggest problem the team is working on now?" "How do people on the team share their work?" "What does success look like in the first three months?"

For engineers, you can ask about tools and habits. "How does code review work here?" "How often do you ship to users?" These show that you already picture yourself doing the job.

Listen to the answer

Q: What do I do after I ask?

A: Listen well. Then ask a short follow-up question, a second question about the answer. For example, "You said the team moves fast. How do you keep the code clean?"

A follow-up question turns the end of the interview into a real talk. It also helps you. You learn if you really want this job. Two or three good questions are enough.

Words to notice

interviewer
the person who asks questions in an interview
formality
something people say or do just to be nice
do your homework
to learn about something before an important event
offer
when a company says it wants to hire you
follow-up question
a second question about the answer you just heard

Think about it

Think of a company you like. What is one question about its team that you cannot answer from its website?
