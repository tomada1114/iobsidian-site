What is the last question?

Q: How do most interviews end?

A: Near the end, the interviewer often says, "Do you have any questions for us?" Many people say, "No, I think you covered everything." That feels polite. But it is a lost chance.

This question is not only a formality, something people say just to be nice. It gives both sides a chance to check what matters. Your questions can show what you care about.

Questions to skip

Q: Are there questions I should not ask?

A: Start by doing your homework. Read the company website before the interview. Instead of only asking what the company makes, ask about something you read. The answer can explain how the public description fits the team.

Pay and time off are fair things to ask about. If these affect whether you can take the job, find out before accepting an offer. A recruiter may be the right person for those questions. You do not need to hide your own needs.

Questions that work

Q: So what should I ask?

A: Ask about the team and the real work. People doing the job can explain details that the website leaves out. Choose questions that help you judge the role. Here are some examples.

"What does a normal week look like on this team?" "What is the biggest problem the team is working on now?" "How do people on the team share their work?" "What does success look like in the first three months?"

For engineers, questions about tools and habits can make the work clearer. Ask, "How does code review work here?" or "How often do you ship to users?" Then listen for a concrete example.

Listen to the answer

Q: What do I do after I ask?

A: Listen well. Then ask a short follow-up question, a second question about the answer. For example, "You said the team moves fast. How do you keep the code clean?"

A follow-up question can turn a short answer into a real talk. You learn more about whether you want this job. If time is short, choose the questions that matter most to your decision.

Words to notice

interviewer
the person who asks questions in an interview
formality
something people say or do just to be nice
do your homework
to learn about something before an important event
offer
when a company says it wants to hire you
follow-up question
a second question about the answer you just heard

English to notice

If + present: “If time is short, choose the questions that matter most to your decision.” If introduces a condition, followed by a suggested action.

Think about it

Use if to explain which question you would ask when interview time is short.
