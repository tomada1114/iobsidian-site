A yawn that jumps

You sit in a meeting. The person next to you opens their mouth wide and yawns. A few seconds later, you yawn too. You did not plan it. It just happened.

This is called a contagious yawn. Contagious means it spreads from one person to another, like a cold. But a yawn does not carry any germs. It spreads through the eyes and the mind.

Are you yawning right now? Many people do when they read about yawns. Just the word can be enough.

Not only people

Yawns can jump between animals, too. Some apes yawn when they see other apes yawn. Dogs are a special case. A dog may yawn after it sees its owner yawn.

This is strange, because a dog and a person are very different animals. It seems that living close together for a long time matters. Dogs watch our faces all day.

What scientists think

Nobody knows the full answer yet. One common idea is about empathy, the skill of feeling what another person feels. People often catch yawns more from family and friends than from strangers. So the yawn may be a small sign that we feel close.

Another idea is about copying. The brain has parts that get active when we watch someone move. They may help us learn by copying others. A yawn may be a simple copy that we cannot stop.

A third idea is about the group. Long ago, humans lived in small groups. If one person felt tired, a yawn could tell the others. Then the whole group could rest at the same time.

Why a yawn at all

Even the first yawn is a question. Old books said we yawn to get more air. Tests did not support this well. Some scientists now think a yawn may help cool the brain a little.

Small children do not catch yawns much. They start to do it a few years later. So catching a yawn may grow with the social mind. For now, the yawn is still a small mystery.
