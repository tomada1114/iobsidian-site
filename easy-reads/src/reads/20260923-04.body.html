A yawn that jumps

You sit in a meeting. The person next to you opens their mouth wide and yawns. A few seconds later, you yawn too. You did not plan it. It just happened.

This is called a contagious yawn. Contagious means it can spread from one person to another. Here it describes copying a behavior, rather than the spread of germs that cause a cold. People may say they catch a yawn after seeing someone else yawn. Seeing or hearing a yawn may lead to another yawn, but not everyone responds.

Are you yawning right now? Many people do when they read about yawns. Just the word can be enough.

Not only people

Yawns can jump between animals, too. Some apes yawn when they see other apes yawn. Dogs are a special case. A dog may yawn after it sees its owner yawn.

A dog can respond to a human yawn even though the two are different animals. That raises a question about how the behavior spreads. The response alone does not tell us what the dog feels or why it yawned.

What scientists think

One idea concerns empathy, the ability to understand another person's feelings. A shared yawn might seem like a sign of closeness. Yet a yawn alone does not measure kindness or prove that two people feel the same thing.

Another idea is about copying. The brain has parts that get active when we watch someone move. They may help us learn by copying others. A yawn may be a simple copy that we cannot stop.

To compare these ideas, researchers need more than a memorable meeting. They can compare reactions to a yawn with reactions to another mouth movement. That helps separate responding to a yawn from simply being tired or watching a face.

Why a yawn at all

Even the first yawn is a question. Old books said we yawn to get more air. Tests did not support this well. Some scientists now think a yawn may help cool the brain a little.

The cause remains a mystery, something we do not fully understand. The first yawn and another person copying it may have different causes. If someone yawns in your meeting, avoid deciding that they are bored or unkind. You can ask whether a break would help, without claiming to know the cause.

Words to notice

contagious
able to spread from one person to another
germs
very small living things that can make you sick
empathy
the ability to understand another person's feelings
catch a yawn
to yawn because you saw someone else yawn
mystery
something that people do not understand yet
lead to / ask whether
cause a result / ask if something is true

English to notice

If clauses: “If someone yawns in your meeting, avoid deciding that they are bored or unkind.” If introduces a situation; the second part offers a response to it.

Think about it

What could you ask if someone seemed tired in a meeting? Use an if clause instead of guessing their feelings.
