The first question

Many job interviews start in the same way. The interviewer smiles and says, "So, tell me about yourself." It sounds easy. But many people do not know where to begin.

Some people start with where they were born. Some talk about school for five minutes. Others just read their resume out loud. None of these is what the interviewer wants.

What they really want

The interviewer is not asking for your life story. They want a short path. The path should end at one point: why you fit this job. Every part of your answer should lead there.

The question also has a second use. It helps you both relax. You talk about something you know well, so it is a good warm-up. The interviewer also hears how you speak and how you organize your ideas.

A good answer is short. One or two minutes is enough. After that, the interviewer can ask about the parts that interest them.

Present, past, future

Many good answers use a simple shape with three parts. First, the present: what you do now. Next, the past: one or two things that brought you here. Last, the future: what you want next, and why this job is a good match.

This shape is easy to remember. It is also easy to follow. The listener always knows where you are in the story.

Here is a short example from a made-up engineer named Ken. "I'm a backend engineer. Right now I build APIs for an online shop. Before that, I worked on a team that moved an old system to the cloud. I learned a lot about keeping services stable. Now I want to work on larger systems. Your team runs services for millions of users, so I think my experience fits well."

Make it your own

Ken does not list every job he had. He picks only the parts that connect to the new role. That is the key skill. You choose, and you leave things out.

Before an interview, write your answer and say it out loud a few times. Then change the future part for each company. The present and the past stay the same, but the ending points to this job.

Words to notice

interviewer
the person who asks questions in a job interview
resume
a short paper that lists your jobs and skills
warm-up
something easy you do before the main part
organize
put things in a clear order
fits
is right for something
