Not first come, first served

Think about a hospital emergency room. People come in all night. Some have a small cut. Some have a bad heart problem. The doctor does not see them in the order they came.

The doctor sees the sickest person first. A new person can come in late and still go to the front. In software, we call this a priority queue. Priority means how important something is. The most important item always comes out first.

One simple rule

How do we build this? We could sort the whole list each time a new item comes in. But that is slow when the list is long. Most programs use a heap instead.

A heap is a tree of items. Each item can have two children below it. The heap keeps only one rule: each parent is bigger than its children. That is all. Two children do not need any order between them.

This one rule is enough. If every parent is bigger than its children, the top item must be the biggest of all. So you can always find the most important item at once. You just look at the top.

Adding a new item

When a new item comes in, we put it at the bottom of the tree. Then we look at its parent. If the new item is bigger, the two items swap places.

We do this again and again. The new item moves up, one step at a time. It stops when its parent is bigger, or when it reaches the top. This is like a very sick person who walks past the line to the front.

Taking the top item out

Now the doctor is free and takes the top item. The top is empty. So we move the last item at the bottom up to the top. It is probably small, so it is in the wrong place.

We compare it with its two children. It swaps with the bigger child. Then it moves down, step by step, until the rule is true again.

Here is the nice part. A tree with a thousand items is only about ten levels tall. So one add or one remove needs only a few swaps. Many languages give you a heap. In Python, it is the heapq module.

Words to notice

emergency room
the part of a hospital for very sick people
priority
how important something is
heap
a tree where each parent is bigger than its children
swap
to change places with each other
at once
right away, with no waiting
