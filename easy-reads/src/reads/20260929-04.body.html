What is a cold?

A cold starts with a virus, a very small thing that can only grow inside living cells. Many different viruses can cause a cold. They get into your nose or throat. Then they start to make copies of themselves.

Here is the surprise. The virus does not do much damage by itself. Most of what feels bad is your body fighting back. Your body finds the virus and sends help to push it out.

Why is my nose running?

When your body finds a virus, it sends more blood to that place. The small blood vessels in your nose get wider. This is called inflammation, a part of the body getting red, warm and swollen.

Your nose also makes much more mucus, the wet, sticky stuff inside it. Mucus catches the virus and helps wash it out. So a runny nose is not the virus winning. It is your body cleaning house.

The same thing makes your nose feel full. The inside of the nose gets swollen, so air cannot move well.

Why does my throat hurt?

Your throat hurts for a similar reason. The body sends help there too, and the throat gets swollen and red. This makes it sore when you eat or talk.

Mucus from your nose can also drip down the back of your throat. That can make you cough. A cough is another way your body pushes things out.

Why am I so tired?

To fight the virus, your body sends out chemical signals. These signals call other cells to help. But they also reach your brain. They tell you to slow down, rest and sleep.

Some of these signals can also give you a fever, a body heat higher than normal. With a cold, a fever is often small or does not come at all.

So the tired feeling has a job. When you rest, your body can use more energy for the fight. After a few days, the virus is gone, and the signals stop. Most people feel better in about a week.
