What is the full feeling?

You are on a plane. It starts to go down, and your ears feel full. Sounds get quiet, as if you have water in your ears. Then you swallow, and you hear a small pop. Now you can hear well again.

The same thing can happen in a car on a mountain road. It can also happen in a fast elevator in a tall building. So what is going on?

Why does air matter?

Air pushes on everything around us. This push is called air pressure. High up, there is less air above you, so the pressure is lower. Down low, the pressure is higher.

Inside your ear there is a thin wall of skin called the eardrum. Sound makes it shake, and that is how you hear. Behind the eardrum is a small space full of air. Air pushes on both sides of the eardrum.

When you go up or down fast, the air outside changes quickly. But the air behind the eardrum stays the same for a while. Now one side pushes harder than the other. The eardrum bends a little and cannot shake freely. That is why sounds feel far away.

Where does the pop come from?

There is a small tube from the space behind your eardrum to the back of your throat. It is called the Eustachian tube. Most of the time, this tube stays closed.

When you swallow or yawn, the tube opens for a moment. Air can move in or out. The pressure on both sides of the eardrum becomes the same again. The eardrum goes back to its normal shape, and you hear a pop.

How can you help it?

You can help the tube open. Swallow a few times, or chew gum. A big yawn works too. Drinking water also makes you swallow.

Babies often cry when a plane lands. Their tubes are small and do not open easily. A drink during landing can help them swallow. When you have a cold, the tube can get blocked, so your ears may hurt more on a flight.
