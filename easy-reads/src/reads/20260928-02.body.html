What is a one-on-one?

At many American tech companies, you have a short meeting with your manager every week or every two weeks. It is just the two of you. People call it a one-on-one, or a "1:1."

It is often about thirty minutes long. It may be in a small room, on a video call, or even on a walk outside.

Who brings the topics?

Many new people wait for the manager to start. They think the manager will ask questions, like a test. But at many companies, the one-on-one is your meeting. You bring the topics.

Some teams keep a shared note for this. Before the meeting, you write down two or three things to talk about. The manager can add things too.

What do people talk about?

A one-on-one is not a status report. Your manager can see your work in other places. So it is better to use the time for things that are hard to say in a group.

Here are some common topics. You can talk about a blocker, something that stops your work. You can ask for feedback on how you are doing. You can talk about what you want to learn next. You can also say when something on the team feels wrong.

Small talk is fine too. Many meetings start with a few minutes about the weekend. This helps you and your manager know each other as people.

What if I have nothing to say?

That happens, and it is OK. You can ask your manager a question instead. "What should I focus on this month?" is a good one. "Is there anything I could do better?" is another.

If you really have nothing, you can end early. Most managers are happy to get time back. But try not to skip it many weeks in a row. The meeting is most useful when things are hard, and it is easier to talk then if you talk often.

Words to notice

one-on-one
a meeting with just two people
status report
a short update on what work is done
blocker
something that stops your work from moving
feedback
what someone thinks about how you did
skip
not do something you usually do
