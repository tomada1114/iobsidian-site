A busy morning

At eight in the morning, the coffee shop was full. Ben stood in line and ordered a hot black coffee. A woman behind him, Jen, asked for an iced latte, a cold coffee drink with milk. They moved to the counter to wait for their drinks.

The shop was noisy. A worker called out names as the cups were ready. Ben heard his name and picked up a cup. It had “Ben” written on it, so he carried it to a small table by the window.

A surprising drink

Ben took a sip. The coffee was cold, sweet, and full of milk. It was an iced latte, not the hot black coffee he had ordered. He checked the cup again. His name was there, but the drink was wrong.

At the next table, Jen looked at a hot black coffee. “This isn’t my latte,” she said. She checked her cup. It had “Jen” on it, but the order was wrong too. The worker had mixed up the drinks.

Check before you swap

Ben and Jen went back to the counter. “I think these drinks were mixed up,” Ben said. He showed the worker what he had ordered. Jen did the same. The worker checked the order slips and saw the mistake.

“Sorry about that,” the worker said. He made the correct drinks right away. Ben and Jen waited nearby. While they were waiting, they talked about the busy morning. They also talked about the park across the street. They did not trade cups after tasting them; they let the shop prepare fresh drinks.

A small lesson

When the new drinks were ready, Ben checked that his was hot and black. Jen checked that her latte was cold. Then they took their drinks and found seats.

They talked for a few minutes about the neighborhood. Ben told Jen that the park was quiet in the morning. She thanked him and went to meet a friend. The next week, Ben saw her in the shop. They smiled and said hello. Now they checked their cups before leaving the counter.

Words to notice

counter
a long table in a shop where people order and pay
call out
say something loudly so people can hear
iced latte
a cold coffee drink with milk
mix up
put things in the wrong order or place
right away
immediately, without waiting

English to notice

While they were waiting, they talked about the busy morning.

The while-clause uses past continuous to describe an action in progress when another past action happened.

Think about it

Tell a short story about two things someone mixed up. Use “while” to say what was happening at the same time.
