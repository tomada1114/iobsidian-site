Gold on the mountains

In late September, the mountains of Colorado change color. The green hills turn bright gold. This happens because of one tree, the aspen. An aspen is a tall, thin tree with white bark and small round leaves.

People visit the mountains to see the fall colors. Some stop by the road, while others walk under the trees. The timing changes with the place and the weather, so a photograph from last year does not tell you exactly when to go.

Why the leaves turn gold

In summer, aspen leaves are green. The green comes from the part of the leaf that makes food from sunlight. When the nights get cold and the days get short, the tree stops making this green part.

The yellow color was in the leaf all summer. The green just covered it. When the green goes away, the yellow and gold can finally show. Soon after, the leaves fall, and the tree rests for the winter.

The leaves also move in a special way. Their stems are flat, so the leaves shake in even a light wind. That is why people call them "quaking aspen." To quake means to shake.

Many trees, one root

Here is the surprising part. An aspen does not only grow from seeds. It sends out long roots under the ground. New trees grow up from these roots. So a hundred trees on a hill can all come from one first tree.

These trees share the same roots. They also share the same genes, the code inside every living thing. Scientists call a group like this a clone, a group of living things with the same genes. In a real way, the whole group is one living thing.

A patch of trees may change color together while nearby trees change later. Different clones can have different leaf colors and timing. That pattern is a clue, but color alone does not show which roots the trees share.

A very old giant

One aspen clone in the state of Utah is very famous. It has a name, Pando. It covers a large area and has tens of thousands of trees. Many people think it is one of the heaviest living things on Earth.

An individual trunk can die while the shared roots continue to grow new trees. The age of one trunk is therefore different from the age of the whole clone. Next time you see a gold hill, look at the patches and ask what might connect them.

Words to notice

aspen
a tall, thin tree with white bark and round leaves
bark
the hard outside skin of a tree
quake
to shake
genes
the code living things pass on to their young

English to notice

While clauses: “An individual trunk can die while the shared roots continue to grow new trees.” While connects two things happening at the same time.

Think about it

Describe two nearby trees or plants. Use while to compare what they do or how they look.
