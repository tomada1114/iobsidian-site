A glue that was too weak

In the late 1960s, a scientist named Spencer Silver worked at 3M, an American company. 3M made tape and many other sticky things. Silver was trying to make a very strong glue.

He made something strange instead. His new glue was weak. It could hold two pieces of paper together, but you could pull them apart easily. And you could stick the paper down again, many times.

Silver thought this glue was interesting. But what was it for? He did not know.

Years with no use

Silver talked about his glue with many people at 3M. He showed it at small meetings inside the company. People listened, but nobody had a good idea for it.

So the glue waited. Years went by. It was an answer, but no one had found the question yet.

A problem at church

Another man at 3M, Art Fry, sang in a church choir, a group that sings together. He used small pieces of paper to mark the songs in his book. But the papers kept falling out.

Fry remembered Silver's weak glue. What if the paper could stick to the page, then come off without hurting it? He tried it, and it worked. His paper markers stayed in place.

Soon people in the office started to use the sticky papers in a new way. They wrote short notes on them and stuck them on reports and desks.

A small yellow square

It still took time to sell the idea. At first, stores did not understand it. But once people tried the notes, they wanted more.

Why yellow? The story goes that the lab next door had some yellow paper, so they used it. The color stayed, and it became the one most people know.

Today, the little yellow squares are in offices and homes all over the world. They began with a glue that did not do what it was made to do.

Words to notice

glue
something wet you use to stick things together
stick
stay on something; hold on to it
choir
a group of people who sing together
markers
small things that show a place in a book
square
a shape with four sides of the same length

Think about it

What small thing on your desk do you use every day? Could you work without it?
