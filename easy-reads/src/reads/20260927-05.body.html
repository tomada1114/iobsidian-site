A trip to the library

Ken moved to a new town in the spring. He did not know many people yet. On a quiet Saturday, he walked to the public library.

The library was big and bright. There were tall shelves and soft chairs. Ken wanted to take some books home. For that, he needed a library card, a small card that lets you borrow books for free.

He went to the front desk. A woman with gray hair smiled at him. "Hi. How can I help you?" she asked.

The missing letter

"I would like a library card, please," Ken said. "This is my first one."

"Great," said the librarian. "I need to see your ID. I also need something with your address on it. A letter or a bill is fine."

Ken showed her his ID. Then he looked in his bag. He had no letter. He had lived in the town for only two weeks. No mail had come to his new home yet.

"I am sorry," he said. "I just moved here. I do not have a letter yet." He felt a little sad. Maybe he had to come back another day.

Another way

The librarian thought for a moment. "Do you have a lease? That is the paper you sign when you rent a home."

"Yes," said Ken. "It is on my phone." He found the file and showed her the screen. It had his name and his new address on it.

"That works," she said. She typed on her computer. A minute later, she gave him a blue plastic card. "Here is your library card. Welcome to town."

His first book

Ken walked around the shelves. He found a book about the history of the town. He brought it back to the desk. "I would like to check out this book," he said. To check out a book means to borrow it from the library.

The librarian scanned his card and the book. She gave him a small paper. "The due date is in three weeks," she said. "That is the last day to bring it back."

"How do I return it?" Ken asked. She pointed to a box by the door. "You can drop it in there, any time of day."

Ken thanked her and walked out into the sun. He had a card, a book, and one new friend in town.
