Is that really me?

You make a short video on your phone. Then you play it back. Your voice sounds strange. It is higher and thinner than you thought. You may even feel a little shy about it.

This happens to almost everyone. Your friends do not hear anything strange. To them, the recording sounds just like you. So why does it sound wrong to you?

Two roads to your ears

Sound is a kind of shaking. When you speak, the air in front of your mouth shakes. That shaking travels through the air to your ears. This is how other people hear you.

But you also hear your voice another way. The shaking starts in your throat. It also moves through the bones of your head. This is called bone conduction, sound that travels through bone, not air. The bone sound goes straight to your inner ear.

So when you speak, you get two sounds at the same time. One comes through the air. One comes through your bones. Your brain mixes them into one voice.

Why it sounds lower

Bone is good at carrying low sounds. It is not so good at carrying high sounds. So the bone sound adds extra low notes to your voice. To you, your voice sounds deep and full.

A recording only catches the sound in the air. It has no bone part. So the low notes you are used to are gone. The voice on the recording sounds higher and thinner to you. But this is the voice other people hear every day.

Getting used to it

You can feel bone sound yourself. Close your ears with your fingers and hum. The hum gets louder, and it feels like it is inside your head. That is your bones at work.

The strange feeling is mostly about habit. You heard the "inside" voice all your life. People who often hear their own recordings, like radio hosts, usually get used to it. After a while, the recorded voice starts to sound normal.
