Why do people tip?

In the US, a tip is extra money you give for good service. You pay it on top of the bill. In many countries, tips are small or not common. In the US, they are a normal part of daily life.

The main reason is pay. Many restaurant servers earn a low base pay. Base pay is the money a worker gets for each hour, before tips. For servers, this pay can be very low. So tips are a big part of what they make each week.

How much should I tip?

At a sit-down restaurant, 15 to 20 percent of the bill is common. A server takes your order and brings your food to the table. If the bill is 50 dollars, a tip of 8 to 10 dollars is normal. Some people tip more for very good service.

Here is an easy way to do the math. Find 10 percent first. Just move the dot one place to the left. Then double it to get 20 percent. Many bills also show tip amounts at the bottom, so you can just pick one.

Who else gets a tip?

Servers are not the only ones. People often tip taxi drivers and hair cutters. They also tip people who bring food to the door. At a hotel, some people leave a few dollars for the room cleaner.

At a coffee shop, the screen may ask you for a tip. Here it is your choice. A small tip is kind, but many people skip it. A tip is most expected when someone serves you for a long time.

What if the service is bad?

Most people still leave a tip, but a smaller one. Remember that the server may not control everything. The kitchen may be slow. If a large problem happens, you can talk to the manager.

Also look at the bill before you tip. Some restaurants add a service charge for big groups. A service charge is a tip that is already on the bill. If you see one, you do not need to add much more.
