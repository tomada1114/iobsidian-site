A number about trust

In the US, many people have a credit score. It is a number that shows how well you pay back money. Banks and other companies look at this number. It helps them decide if they can trust you.

The number is often between 300 and 850. A high number is good. A low number means you had problems paying back in the past.

This number matters in daily life. You may need it to get a credit card or a car loan, money you borrow and pay back later. Some people who rent homes also check it.

Built from your history

So where does the number come from? It comes from your history. Each time you borrow money, companies keep a record. They write down if you paid on time or late.

These records go to a few large companies called credit bureaus. A credit bureau is a company that collects your payment records. Then a math model turns your records into one number.

The most important part is simple. Did you pay on time? Paying on time, again and again, makes the number go up. Late payments make it go down.

Why newcomers start with none

Here is the surprise for many new arrivals. You do not start with a low score. You start with no score. The system has no records about you yet.

Your history from your home country does not come with you. Maybe you paid every bill on time for twenty years. The US system cannot see that. To it, you are a new person.

Think of it like a new user account in an app. The account works, but it has no data yet. The system cannot say anything about you.

Starting from zero

Many newcomers start with a small, simple card. One common type is a secured card. You give the bank some money first, and that money is your limit.

Then you use the card a little and pay the full bill on time. Month by month, the records grow. After some months, the system has enough history. Then you get your first score.
