One click, one color

Most drawing apps have a paint bucket tool. You pick a color and click inside a shape. The whole shape turns that color at once.

It looks like the app knows where the shape is. But it does not. A picture on a screen is only a grid of small dots. Each dot is called a pixel, and each pixel has one color.

So the tool does something simple. It starts at one pixel and spreads to its neighbors, one step at a time.

From one pixel to the next

First, the tool looks at the pixel you clicked. Say it is white. Now the tool has one rule: paint white pixels, and stop at any other color.

It paints the first pixel. Then it looks at the four pixels next to it: up, down, left and right. Each white neighbor gets painted too. A black line is not white, so the tool stops there.

Then the tool looks at the neighbors of those new pixels. And then at their neighbors. The color grows out like water on a flat table. When no white pixels are left to reach, the job is done.

A line of pixels to visit

How does the tool remember where to go next? It keeps a list, often called a queue. A queue works like a line at a store. The first one in is the first one out.

The tool takes a pixel from the front of the line. It paints it and adds its white neighbors to the back. It does this again and again until the line is empty. Programmers call this a BFS, or breadth-first search.

This is also why a small gap matters. If the black line has a hole of just one pixel, the color walks right through it. Then it fills the whole page.

The same idea in other places

This way of spreading shows up in many programs. A game can use it to find all the land a player can walk to. A map app can use a similar idea to find a short way from one place to another.

The next time you click the paint bucket, think of the small line of pixels. Each one waits for its turn.

Words to notice

pixel
one very small dot of color on a screen
neighbors
the things right next to something
queue
a line where the first one in goes out first
spreads
moves out to cover more space
gap
a small open space in something
