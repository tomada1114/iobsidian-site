A cloud from your mouth

It is a cold morning. You step outside and breathe out. A small white cloud comes out of your mouth. Then it is gone.

In summer, this does not happen. You breathe the same way, but you see nothing. So what changes? The answer is not in your breath. It is in the air around you.

Water you cannot see

Your body is warm and wet inside. When air goes into your lungs, it picks up heat and water. So the air you breathe out is warm, and it carries a lot of water.

But this water is not drops. It is a gas called water vapor. Water vapor is clear, like air. You cannot see it at all. Your breath always has it, in summer and in winter.

Warm air can hold a lot of water vapor. Cold air can hold much less. This one fact explains the white cloud.

Cold air does the work

When you breathe out on a cold day, your warm breath meets cold air. It cools down very fast. Now it holds too much water vapor for its new temperature.

The extra water has to go somewhere. It turns back into tiny drops of liquid water. This change is called condensation, when a gas turns into a liquid. Each drop is very small, but there are many of them.

Together, the tiny drops catch light and look white. That is your little cloud. In fact, real clouds in the sky form in the same way. Warm, wet air goes up, cools down, and makes tiny drops.

Why it goes away

The cloud does not stay long. Your breath mixes with the dry air around it. The tiny drops evaporate, or turn back into gas. Then you cannot see them again.

You can see the same thing in other places. Steam over a cup of hot tea is not really steam. It is tiny drops, made the same way. A cold glass gets wet on the outside for the same reason too. The water comes from the air, not from the glass.

Think about it

Where else have you seen tiny water drops appear from the air? What was cold, and what was warm?
