A different kind of interview

Some companies give software engineers a system design interview. The interviewer asks you to plan a service, such as a short-link website or a chat app. You may talk for about an hour, but the format and time can change by company and role.

You might draw boxes on a whiteboard or shared screen. The boxes show parts of the service, such as a computer that handles requests, a database, and a cache. There may be little or no coding.

There is no single drawing

Two engineers can make different designs for the same service. Both may work well. The interviewer is not always checking your drawing against one secret answer.

Instead, they want to hear how you think. Can you make a large problem easier to understand? Can you explain why one choice may be better than another? Speaking clearly helps the interviewer follow your ideas.

Start with questions

Start with questions about what people need. How many people might use the service? Do they read information more often than they change it? Must the information be correct right away? The answers help you choose a useful design.

If a service is for a small group, it may need fewer parts than one used by millions of people. You do not need to guess everything. Ask the interviewer to explain anything unclear, then say which needs you will plan for first.

Talk through each choice

A trade-off is a choice with a benefit and a cost. For example, a cache keeps copies of information so a service can answer some requests quickly. The copy may be a little old, so the service must decide when to update it.

To talk through your choice, you could say, “I will use a cache because most people read the data more often than they change it. The cost is that a few people may see an old copy for a short time.” This shows the interviewer what matters to you and why. There can be several good choices, so explain your reasons and listen to new information.

Words to notice

interviewer
the person who asks questions in an interview
cache
a fast place that keeps copies of information
trade-off
a choice with a benefit and a cost
start with
begin by doing this before other things
talk through
explain an idea or choice as you go

English to notice

because most people read the data more often than they change it

Use because plus a subject and verb to explain a reason.

Think about it

Choose a service you know. What would you start with, and which trade-off would you talk through? Give one reason with “because.”
