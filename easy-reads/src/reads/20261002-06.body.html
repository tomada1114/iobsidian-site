A calm voice on TV

In the 1980s and 1990s, many people in America watched a quiet painting show. It was called The Joy of Painting. The painter was Bob Ross. He had big curly hair and a soft, kind voice.

In each show, he started with a white canvas. A canvas is the cloth that painters paint on. About half an hour later, the canvas showed a full landscape: mountains, trees, a lake and a sky. People watched the whole picture grow in one show.

Before the show

Bob Ross was born in Florida. As a young man, he joined the US Air Force. He lived in Alaska for many years. There he saw snowy mountains and deep green forests. These places came back again and again in his paintings.

In Alaska, he started to paint in his free time. He also learned a fast way to paint from another TV painter. Later, he left the Air Force and taught painting full time. Then his own TV show began.

Wet paint on wet paint

How could he finish so fast? He used a way of painting called wet-on-wet. Most oil painters wait for one layer of paint to dry. Then they add the next layer. Oil paint can take days to dry, so this takes a long time.

Bob Ross did not wait. He put new paint on top of wet paint. The colors mixed right on the canvas. With a big brush, he made a soft sky in a few moves. With a knife, he made sharp mountain tops. One tree took only a few seconds.

This way is fast, but it is not new. Painters used it long before him. He just showed it to millions of people in a clear and simple way.

Happy little accidents

Sometimes a drop of paint went to the wrong place. Bob Ross did not get upset. He called it a happy little accident. Then he turned the mistake into a tree or a rock.

This idea helped many viewers. They felt they could try painting too, even without training. Many people bought brushes and started to paint at home. Others just watched to relax. His shows are still easy to find and watch today.

Words to notice

canvas
the cloth that painters paint on
landscape
a picture of land, like mountains, trees and lakes
wet-on-wet
putting new paint on paint that is still wet
layer
one flat part that lies on top of another
happy little accident
a small mistake that turns into something good
