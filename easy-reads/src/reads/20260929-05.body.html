A wet morning

It was raining hard on Monday morning. Mia stood at the bus stop with her coat pulled close. Her hair was already wet. She did not have an umbrella.

An old man sat on the bench next to her. He had a gray hat and a long black umbrella. The umbrella was old, with a wooden handle. He held it close, like a friend.

"Bad day for the bus," he said with a smile. "It is always late when it rains." Mia laughed. "Then I will be late too," she said.

The forgotten umbrella

A bus came, but it was not Mia's bus. The old man stood up slowly. He waved to her and got on. The doors closed with a loud sound.

Then Mia looked at the bench. The black umbrella was still there. He had left it behind. She picked it up and ran after the bus.

"Wait! Wait!" she called. Her shoes hit the puddles, small pools of water on the street. But the bus did not stop. It turned the corner and was gone.

Mia stood in the rain, out of breath. She had the umbrella now. But she did not know the man's name. She did not know where he lived.

The next morning

Mia did not open the umbrella that day. It did not feel like hers. She took it to work and put it next to her desk. At home, she stood it by the door.

The next morning, the sky was clear. Mia went to the bus stop a little early. She carried the black umbrella under her arm. She had a small hope.

The old man was there, on the same bench. He looked sad and a little lost. Then he saw the umbrella, and his face changed. "My umbrella!" he said.

A small gift back

"You forgot it yesterday," Mia said. "I ran after your bus, but I was too slow." The old man took the umbrella with both hands. He held the wooden handle for a long time.

"My wife gave me this," he said quietly. "Many years ago. I was so worried." Mia felt a warm feeling in her chest. She was glad she had kept it safe.

The next week, it rained again. Mia came to the bus stop without an umbrella, as usual. The old man was waiting. He opened the big black umbrella and held it over them both. "Plenty of room," he said.
