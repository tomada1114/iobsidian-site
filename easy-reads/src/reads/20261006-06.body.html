The report nobody liked

Leo and Hana worked on a small team that sent a report to their manager every Friday. The report was a short file that showed how many tickets the team had closed that week. It was not hard work, but it was boring, so they both kept forgetting it.

One Friday, Leo was copying numbers from the ticket system into a spreadsheet when Hana walked over. "Did you send the report yet?" she asked. "I am doing it now," he said. "This part always takes the longest."

A big plan that waited

A few months earlier, Hana had written a big plan. It was a design for a tool that would collect the data, make charts, write the report and send it by email. The plan was good, but nobody had time to build it. It was still waiting in a folder, which nobody opened anymore.

"Maybe we should finally build your tool," Leo said. Hana laughed. "We said that last month too. I think the whole tool is too much for us right now."

Only the first step

That weekend, Leo thought about the steps that took the most time. Copying the numbers was the worst one. It was the step that he repeated every single week, and it was easy to make mistakes there.

On Monday, he wrote a tiny script, a small program that does one job by itself. It did only one thing: it got the numbers from the ticket system and put them into the spreadsheet. It did not make charts or send email. It took him about an hour to write and test.

The next Friday, the script ran in a few seconds. Leo still checked the numbers, wrote two lines of comments and sent the report himself. But he saved time, about ten minutes, and he did not have to copy anything by hand.

What came next

Ten minutes is not a lot, but the small win changed how the team felt. Hana looked at the script and said, "Now I can see what to do next. The chart is the next boring step." They decided to improve the report a step at a time, instead of waiting for the perfect tool.

A small automation that works is better than a big plan that waits, and it also shows you the next step. If you want to try this, write down one task that you repeat every week. Then circle the single step that is most boring, and think only about that step.

Words and phrases to notice

a step at a time
slowly, doing one part and then the next
saved time
needed less time to do something; e.g. "The list saved time."
script
a small program that does one job
by hand
done by a person, not by a machine
small win
a little success that gives you energy

English to notice

Was copying ... when: “Leo was copying numbers from the ticket system into a spreadsheet when Hana walked over.” The long action was in progress, and a short event happened in the middle of it.

That / which: “It was the step that he repeated every single week.” The clause after “that” tells us which step. In “a folder, which nobody opened anymore,” “which” adds extra information after a comma.

Think about it

What is one task that you repeat every week? Which step is the most boring, and how could you save time on it? Try using “that” or “a step at a time” in your answer.
