A hill of gold

In the Colorado mountains, fall comes early. The air gets cold at night. Then the aspen trees change color. Their small round leaves turn bright yellow and gold.

If you look at a hill of aspens, you may see something strange. One large group of trees turns gold at the same time. The group next to it is still green. It looks like someone painted the hill in big blocks.

Many trees, one plant

Here is the reason. A group of aspens is often one plant. The trees look separate above the ground. But under the ground, they share the same roots.

An aspen can send new shoots up from its roots. A shoot is a young plant that grows up out of the ground. Each shoot becomes a new tree. Over many years, one plant can make hundreds or thousands of trees.

All these trees have the same genes, the plan inside each living thing. Scientists call a group like this a clone. Every tree in the clone is a copy of the others.

Same plan, same time

Trees in one clone share genes, so their leaves often change at similar times. Genes affect how they respond to shorter days and cold weather. In spring, their new leaves may also appear together.

A nearby clone may have different genes and change at another time. But color alone does not prove that trees share roots. Sunlight and local weather can also affect the leaves. Blocks of color are a clue to what may be under the ground.

You can think of it like one program running on many machines. If the code, inputs and conditions match, the outputs may match too. Shared code alone does not tell you everything about a machine.

A very old giant

One aspen clone in Utah has a name. People call it Pando. It has tens of thousands of trees. It is one of the heaviest living things in the world.

An individual tree can die while the shared roots stay alive. Those roots can send up a new shoot to take its place. So the age of one trunk does not tell us the age of the whole clone. The part we see is only one part of the living system.

Words to notice

aspen
a tall, thin tree with small round leaves
shoot
a young plant that grows up out of the ground
genes
the plan inside each living thing
clone
a living thing that is a copy of another
roots
the parts of a plant under the ground
respond to / take its place
react to something / replace it

English to notice

If + present: “If the code, inputs and conditions match, the outputs may match too.” If gives the conditions; may shows a possible result.

Think about it

Use if and may to describe what a block of gold leaves could tell you.
