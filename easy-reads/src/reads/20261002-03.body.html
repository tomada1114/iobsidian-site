A school for almost anyone

In the US, you often hear the words community college. It is a school for adults after high school. Most towns and cities have one, or there is one not far away.

A community college is open to almost anyone. You do not need very high grades to get in. Most programs take two years. The cost is much lower than at a big university.

Start small, then move on

Here is something many people do not know. A lot of Americans start at a community college to save money. They take basic classes there for two years. Then they transfer, or move, to a four-year university.

At the university, they finish the last two years. In the end, they get the same degree as other students. A degree is the paper that shows you finished college. But they paid much less for the first half.

This plan has other good points too. Classes are often small. Students can live at home. Some people also work while they study.

Classes for a job

Not everyone wants to go on to a university. Many students come to learn a skill for work. They study to be a nurse's helper, a cook or a car mechanic, a person who fixes cars.

There are tech classes too. You can learn basic coding, networks or how to fix computers. Some classes are at night, so people with day jobs can come.

Classes just for fun

Some adults take only one class. They do not want a degree. They want to learn to paint, cook, dance or speak Spanish. Older people often join after they stop working.

For many of them, the class is also a way to meet people. You see the same faces each week. You talk before class starts. Over time, some of them become friends.

So a community college is many things. It can be the first step to a degree, a road to a new job or a fun night out.
