The same face every night

On a night with a full Moon, look at its dark and light shapes. Some people see a face. Some see a rabbit.

Now look again next month. The shapes are the same. Look again in ten years. They are still the same. People all through history saw these same shapes.

So does the Moon never turn? Many people think so. But the answer is no. The Moon does turn. It just turns in a very special way.

One turn, one trip

The Moon does two things at the same time. It goes around Earth. This trip is called an orbit, the path one thing takes around another. One orbit takes about four weeks.

The Moon also spins, like a ball on a finger. One full spin also takes about four weeks. The spin and the trip take the same time. Because of this, one side of the Moon always faces Earth.

Try this at home. Walk in a circle around a chair. Always keep your face toward the chair. When you get back to the start, you have also turned once. But the chair only saw your face. It never saw the back of your head.

Why the speeds match

This is not luck. It comes from gravity, the pull between two objects. Earth pulls on the Moon, and the Moon pulls on Earth.

Long ago, the Moon spun faster. Earth's pull changed its shape as it turned. These changes released energy as heat and slowed the spin. Over time, one spin came to take as long as one trip around Earth. Scientists call this tidal locking.

Many other moons are locked in the same way. Most large moons of other planets show one face to their planet.

The other side

People often call the other half the "dark side" of the Moon. But that side gets sunlight too. It has day and night, like our side. We call it the far side because it faces away from Earth. The name describes its position, not its light.

For a long time, people could not see most of the far side. Then, in 1959, a space probe called Luna 3 took its first photos. A space probe is a machine sent into space without people inside. The photos showed many more holes and fewer dark flat areas.

Words to notice

orbit

gravity

English to notice

Because + clause: “We call it the far side because it faces away from Earth.” Because introduces a clause that gives the reason.
