A question before building

When you build a product, it is easy to keep adding things. A new button, a new setting, a new screen. Each one feels like progress, but more features do not always mean more value.

There is a simple question that can help before you add anything: "If this disappeared tomorrow, what would you miss?" You ask your users, not yourself. What they say often shows the real value of the product, and it is often different from the list of features you are proud of.

The boring feature

Imagine a developer who has made a small tool for her team. It turns long log files into short reports. She has spent weeks on a colorful chart page, and she plans to add a search function next.

First, she asks three people the question. None of them talk about the charts. All three say almost the same thing: "I would miss the daily email." Every morning, the tool sends a short message that says whether anything went wrong overnight. She built that email in one afternoon and almost forgot about it.

Her users had come to count on that email. To count on something means to trust that it will be there when you need it. They checked it with their coffee and started their day calmly. They took it for granted, which means they used it every day without thinking about it, so they never mentioned it in feedback.

What is built and what is valued

This story shows a useful difference. What you have built is a list of features. What people value is the small number of things they would feel sad or worried to lose. The two lists are rarely the same.

The answer also helps you decide what to protect. If the developer had changed the email format, or moved it to a weekly schedule, she could have hurt the part people liked most. Now she knows to keep it reliable, and maybe to improve it, before she spends time on search.

Asking "what would you miss?" works better than asking "what do you want?" People are not always good at guessing what they would use. They are much better at noticing what is already part of their routine.

Try it with three people

You do not need a big survey to understand your value. Ask three users one question: "If this tool stopped working tomorrow, what would you miss first?" Write down their exact words, not your summary of them.

A few honest answers can show you what to protect and what to improve. Then your next feature can be built on the thing people already count on.

Words and phrases to notice

count on
trust that something will be there; "I count on my alarm."
took it for granted
used it without noticing its value
feature
one thing a product can do
overnight
during the night
routine
the usual things you do every day

English to notice

Second conditional: "If this disappeared tomorrow, what would you miss?" We use a past verb after "if" and "would" in the answer to talk about an imagined situation.

Noun clauses with what: "What people value is the small number of things..." Here "what people value" works like one noun. It means "the things that people value."

Think about it

Think of a tool or app you use every day. If it stopped working tomorrow, what would you miss first? Try to use "would" and "count on" in your answer.
