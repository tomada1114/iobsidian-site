A common question

In many job interviews, you hear questions like this: "Tell me about a time when something went wrong." Or: "Tell me about a hard problem you solved." The interviewer wants a real story from your past work.

These questions are hard because there is no right answer. Many people talk for too long. Others give a short answer with no details. A simple plan can help. It is called the STAR method.

Four parts of a story

STAR stands for four words. Situation is where and when the story happens. Task is the job you had to do. Action is what you did. Result is what happened in the end.

Here is the key point. Most of your time should go to Action. Situation and Task are only the setup. Keep them short, maybe two or three sentences each.

Also, tell the Action part with "I", not "we". The team may have worked together. But the interviewer wants to hire you, not your team. So they need to know what you did.

One made-up example

Say the question is: "Tell me about a time you fixed a big problem." Here is a made-up answer from a software engineer.

Situation: "Our app got very slow every Monday morning. Users sent many complaints." Task: "My manager asked me to find the cause and fix it in one week."

Action: "First, I looked at the server logs, the records the server keeps. I saw that one database query took ten seconds. Then I found out why. A big report ran at the same time every Monday. I moved the report to Sunday night. I also added an index to the table, so the query could find rows faster. Last, I set up an alert, a message that tells us when the app gets slow."

Result: "The query went from ten seconds to under one second. The Monday complaints stopped."

Why this works

Look at the example again. The Action part is the longest. It has clear steps, and each step starts with "I". The Result has a number, so it is easy to believe.

You can plan a few STAR stories before an interview. Then you can use them for many different questions.

Words to notice

interviewer
the person who asks questions in a job interview
logs
records that a computer keeps about what happened
query
a request to get data from a database
alert
a message that warns you about a problem
setup
the first part that prepares for the main part

Think about it

Think of one problem you solved at work or school. What was your Action? Try to tell it with "I".
