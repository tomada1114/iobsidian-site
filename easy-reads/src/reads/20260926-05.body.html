A dog without a tag

On Saturday, Sam walked through the farmers market to buy apples and bread. A small brown dog stood near the apple table. It wore a red collar, but there was no tag with a name or phone number.

Sam noticed a white mark on one of the dog's front paws. He did not reach for it. Instead, he told the person at the apple table what he had seen and asked, “Could you ask around?”

Ask around

The seller checked with two nearby workers, then called the information booth. While one worker made the call, Sam stayed near the apple table to keep an eye on the dog from a little distance.

A staff member came over and guided the dog to a quiet, closed area near the booth. The worker made a short announcement: a small brown dog with a red collar was safe at the information booth. The owner could come there to ask about it. The announcement did not mention the white mark, so the family could describe that detail in person.

A familiar name

After a few minutes, a woman arrived with a child. The child called, “Pepper!” The dog looked up and wagged its tail, but the staff member asked the woman to describe the dog first.

The woman showed a photo of Pepper with the same red collar and white mark on her paw. The details matched. The staff member opened the gate, and Pepper ran to the child.

Everyone helps a little

The woman thanked Sam and the apple seller. Pepper's tag had fallen off earlier that week, so the family planned to get a new one before going home. The child held Pepper's leash while the woman checked the phone number she would put on the new tag.

Sam returned to the apple table for his bag. The seller asked if he had found the dog's family. “Yes,” Sam said. “Everyone helped a little.” Then he carried the apples home.

Words to notice

collar
a band that a dog wears around its neck
tag
a small piece with a name or number
information booth
a place where people can ask for help
ask around
ask several people for information
keep an eye on
watch something carefully

English to notice

While one worker made the call, Sam stayed near the apple table to keep an eye on the dog from a little distance.

The while-clause tells what was happening at the same time as the next action.

Think about it

If you found something that belonged to someone else, who would you ask for help? Use “ask around” or “keep an eye on.”
