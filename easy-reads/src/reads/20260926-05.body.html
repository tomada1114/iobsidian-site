A dog with no name

Every Saturday, Sam went to the farmers market near his home. He liked the fresh bread and the tomatoes. This Saturday was sunny, and the market was busy. People walked slowly between the tables.

Sam stopped to buy some apples. Then he felt something warm on his foot. He looked down. A small brown dog sat there and looked up at him.

"Hello," Sam said. "Where is your family?" The dog had a red collar, a thin band around its neck. But there was no tag on it. There was no name and no phone number.

Asking for help

Sam picked up the dog. It was light and a little scared. He walked to the apple table. "Is this your dog?" he asked the woman there. She shook her head. "No, but I saw it near the flower table a few minutes ago."

Sam went to the flower table. The man there did not know the dog either. But he had an idea. "Go to the info booth," he said. "It is the small tent by the gate. They have a microphone."

On the way, a girl gave Sam a small piece of cheese for the dog. The dog ate it fast and wagged its tail. Sam smiled. The dog was not so scared now.

A voice across the market

At the info booth, an old man listened to Sam's story. Then he took the microphone. "We found a small brown dog with a red collar," he said. "Please come to the info booth by the gate."

Sam waited with the dog in his arms. Five minutes went by. Then ten. He started to worry. What if the owner was not at the market?

Then he heard running feet. A woman hurried toward the booth with a child. The child shouted, "Pepper!" The dog jumped out of Sam's arms and ran to her.

Pepper goes home

The woman was out of breath. "Thank you so much," she said. "Her tag fell off last week. We were going to buy a new one today." She laughed a little. "I think we will buy it right now."

The child hugged Pepper. Then she gave Sam a thank you card from her bag. It had a drawing of a dog on it. Sam put the card in his pocket.

He went back to buy his bread. The woman at the apple table waved. "Did you find the owner?" she asked. "Yes," Sam said. "Everyone helped a little."
