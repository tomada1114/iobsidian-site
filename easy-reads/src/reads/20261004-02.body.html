Why do I forget my own work?

When you finish a task, the details feel clear. You know what was wrong, what you tried and why it worked. But a few months later, most of that is gone. The work is done, and the story behind it is often forgotten.

This becomes a problem when someone asks, "What have you learned this year?" You may answer, "I fixed some bugs," but that does not show much. Skills are hard to explain from memory, because the useful part is the details: the problem, the choice you made and the result.

What does a good note look like?

Imagine a software engineer who has just fixed a slow page. Users waited several seconds for a list to load. She tried a few ideas and found that the page asked the database the same question many times. When she changed it to ask once, the page loaded much faster.

At the time, this felt too small to write down. But a three-line note keeps the important part:

  • Problem: the list page was slow to load.
  • What I tried: I found repeated database calls and joined them into one.
  • What changed: the page now loads quickly.

The note does not need good writing. It only needs to be honest and written right away, while the facts are fresh.

How do the notes help later?

Notes like this help you keep track of your growth. When you update a portfolio, a collection of work that shows your skills, you can pick a few clear examples instead of guessing. In a conversation about work, you can say how a problem was solved, not just that it was solved.

They also help on hard days. When you feel you have not learned anything, you can read your notes and see the many things you have fixed. Even a task that went badly can teach something, so it is worth writing about too.

How can I start?

Keep it small. After your next finished task, write three lines: the problem, what you tried and what changed. Put them in one simple file, so you can find them later. It takes about two minutes.

One note will not change much. But small notes add up to a clear record of what you can do, and that record is ready when you need it.

Words and phrases to notice

keep track of
follow and record something over time, e.g. keep track of your spending
at the time
at that moment in the past, e.g. at the time, I was busy
portfolio
a collection of work that shows your skills
fresh
new and still clear in your mind
add up to
slowly become something bigger together

English to notice

Present perfect: “What have you learned this year?” We use have + past participle for actions in a time that continues to now.

Passive voice: “The story behind it is often forgotten.” The passive puts the focus on the thing, not on the person who acts.

Think about it

What is one problem you have fixed recently? Describe it in three lines, and try to use “have fixed” or “was solved” in your answer.
