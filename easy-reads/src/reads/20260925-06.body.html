Loading ships by hand

Think about a port in the early 1950s. A ship is waiting to take goods across the sea. The goods come in bags, boxes and barrels of every size.

Workers carry each item onto the ship by hand. They pack it in a small space with care. Then, at the next port, other workers take it all out again, one piece at a time.

This work was slow and hard. Loading one ship could take days. Ships often spent more time in port than at sea. Some goods also broke or went missing on the way.

A trucker with an idea

Malcolm McLean ran a trucking company in the United States. His trucks often waited at ports while workers loaded ships. He thought this was a waste of time and money.

His idea was simple. Why lift every item? Why not lift the whole back of the truck and put it on the ship? The goods could stay inside the box from start to end.

In 1956, a ship he used carried the first of these boxes. The box is called a shipping container, a large steel box that can move by truck, train or ship.

One size for everyone

At first, different companies made boxes in different sizes. This was a problem. A box from one company did not fit the ships or trains of another.

Over time, people agreed on a standard, one set of sizes that everyone uses. The corners of every box were made the same way. Now any crane, a big machine that lifts heavy things, could pick up any box.

Software people know this idea well. It is like a common interface. When everyone uses the same shape, all the parts work together.

Faster and cheaper

With containers, a crane lifts one box after another. A ship can load in hours, not days. Fewer goods break, and fewer go missing, because the box stays closed.

Moving goods became much cheaper. Companies could make things in one country and sell them far away. Today, many of the things in your home came across the sea in a steel box.

Words to notice

port
a place where ships stop to load and unload
goods
things that people buy and sell
shipping container
a large steel box for moving goods
standard
one set of sizes or rules that everyone uses
crane
a big machine that lifts heavy things
