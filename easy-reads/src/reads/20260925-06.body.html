Loading ships by hand

In the early 1950s, ships carried goods in bags, boxes, and barrels of many sizes. This cargo was loaded piece by piece. Today, this kind of cargo is called breakbulk cargo. Workers carried each item onto a ship, then carried it off again at the next port.

Loading a ship could take several days. Workers had to fit each item into the space they had. Goods could also break or go missing while being moved. The ship spent a long time waiting at the port.

A box for the whole trip

Malcolm McLean ran a trucking company in the United States. His trucks often waited while workers moved goods onto ships. He thought that the goods could stay in one large box as they traveled. Then a crane could lift the box from a truck onto a ship.

On April 26, 1956, the converted tanker SS Ideal X sailed from Port Newark with 58 metal containers. They were loaded in less than eight hours. Loading loose cargo could take days. The containers stayed closed as they moved, so workers did not have to take out every item at each stop.

Agree on shared sizes

At first, companies used containers with different sizes and fittings. A container that worked with one ship or crane might not fit another. Companies needed a shared standard, a set of rules for how equipment should be made.

In 1961, ISO created a group to develop shared rules for freight containers. Common sizes and corner fittings helped cranes lift the boxes and helped containers fit into ships, trucks, and trains. Each part of the journey could be built to work with the same container.

Many parts, one system

A crane can move one container at a time. Workers do not have to carry every bag or box one by one. The same sealed container can travel from a ship to a train or truck. Shared rules make these changes easier.

This is like using a common connection between parts of a computer system. Different teams can build the parts, but they agree on how the parts fit together. A shipping container is a simple example of one shared shape helping a large system work.

Words to notice

breakbulk cargo
goods moved as separate bags, boxes, or barrels
container
a large box used to move goods
standard
a shared set of rules for how something is made
crane
a tall machine that lifts heavy things
one by one
separately, with one thing after another
fit into
be the right size or shape for a space

English to notice

They were loaded in less than eight hours.

“Were loaded” is passive. It focuses on the containers and what happened to them.

Think about it

What moves through several steps in a system? Explain how it is handled one by one, or why it can fit into the next step.
