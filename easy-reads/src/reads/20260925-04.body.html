Tears in the kitchen

You stand in the kitchen and cut an onion. After a minute, your eyes start to hurt. Then the tears come. You are not sad at all.

Almost everyone knows this feeling. But why does it happen? The answer is a small bit of chemistry. It starts inside the onion itself.

What happens when you cut

An onion is made of many tiny cells. A cell is the smallest living part of a plant. Inside the cells, the onion keeps some special things apart from each other.

When you cut the onion, your knife breaks many cells open. The things that were apart now meet and mix. They start a quick chemical reaction, a change that makes something new.

The new thing is a gas. It is light, so it goes up into the air. And your face is right above the onion.

Why your eyes make tears

The gas reaches your eyes. Your eyes are always a little wet. When the gas touches this water, it bothers your eyes. They start to sting, a small, sharp pain.

Your body does not like this. So your eyes make more tears. The tears try to wash the gas away. This is the same thing your eyes do when dust gets in them.

So the tears are not a problem. They are your body's way to protect your eyes. They just come at a bad time.

Two simple tricks

First, put the onion in the fridge before you cut it. A cold onion makes the reaction slow. Less gas goes up into the air.

Second, use a sharp knife. A sharp knife cuts cleanly and breaks fewer cells. A dull knife, one that is not sharp, crushes the onion. More cells break, and more gas comes out.

These tricks do not stop the tears for good. But they can make your time in the kitchen a little easier.
